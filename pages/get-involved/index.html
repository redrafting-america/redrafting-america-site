Careers & Leadership

This page presents the proposed Eleven Pillars leadership model and the officer or director responsible for each pillar: vision, continuity, history, resources, law, community, knowledge, sustainability, integrity, innovation, and execution. It includes a full organizational diagram, detailed department functions for every role, a founding-role inquiry link, and a clear notice that the organization is pre-launch and all listed opportunities are currently volunteer roles rather than paid positions. Why it matters: prospective leaders can see both the scale of the proposed structure and the responsibilities attached to each position.

Content outline

Partnerships

The current outline defines the standards for future relationships with institutions, nonprofits, educators, researchers, and civic groups. It calls for every partnership to serve a stated public benefit and project need, preserve the organization’s independence from donor, partner, or political control, and begin with a defined inquiry process. No partnership application or directory is currently published. Why it matters: collaboration can expand capability only when authority, ethical boundaries, and public accountability remain clear.

Content outline

Donate

The current page explicitly states that no donation mechanism will be presented until the required organizational, legal, financial, and payment safeguards are satisfied. Its outline identifies the disclosures that future giving will require: approved uses of funds, receipts and tax status, donor privacy, and public funding transparency. Why it matters: contributions should not be accepted until they can be handled securely, described accurately, and reported without compromising the project’s independence.

Contact

The homepage provides the organization’s current business email and directs it toward purposeful organizational communication. The planned Contact section distinguishes general administration, partnerships, future donation questions, media inquiries, and public deliberation; constitutional debate and proposals are intended for the future Convention or approved public channels rather than business email. Why it matters: visitors need a clear way to reach the organization while understanding which conversations belong in public civic forums.
