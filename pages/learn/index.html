Content outline

Constitutional Glossary

The current outline separates familiar constitutional and legal terms from institutions and vocabulary created specifically for Constitution v2.0. It also calls for links from each definition to the Articles and Explainers where the term is used and for definitions to be revised alongside the governing text. The glossary entries have not yet been populated. Why it matters: informed public participation requires a shared vocabulary that is consistent across the project and understandable without legal training.

Content outline

Plain-Language Guides

The current outline defines accessible guides for Article summaries, government institutions, constitutional rights, alternative reading and comprehension needs, and future translation. These guides are intended to simplify presentation without changing the meaning or omitting material limits and tradeoffs. The guide library has not yet been published. Why it matters: constitutional design belongs to the public, and the official material should be usable by readers with different backgrounds and levels of expertise.

Content outline

Case Studies

The current outline divides future case studies into institutional successes, institutional failures, and relevant lessons from other constitutional democracies. It also establishes an evidence standard that separates documented facts from the project’s own conclusions. No completed case studies are currently listed. Why it matters: proposed reforms should be tested against real institutional experience and comparative evidence rather than intuition or rhetoric alone.

Content outline

Teaching Resources

The current outline defines materials for educators, civic organizations, reading groups, and structured public discussions. Planned resources include discussion guides, modular lesson materials, suggested reading sequences through the Constitution and Explainers, facilitator notes for fair evidence-based conversation, and accessible formats. Why it matters: durable constitutional understanding must be teachable beyond this website and should not require political agreement to study seriously.

Content outline

Frequently Asked Questions

The current outline organizes future answers around the organization, Constitution v2.0, the Article Explainers, public participation, and funding. It is designed to address recurring questions about legal and drafting status, what is available now, what remains future work, and how the organization supports and explains its decisions. Why it matters: answering difficult or skeptical questions directly is essential to credibility and productive public debate.
