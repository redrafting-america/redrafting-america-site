Content outline

Our Story

The current outline is organized around the project’s origins, its evolution from an earlier technology concept into a focused constitutional initiative, its present direction, and a future historical record tied to dated milestones and approved public records. It is intended to give readers the sequence and context behind the work before they evaluate the organization’s current proposals. Why it matters: constitutional reform should be presented with a traceable history, including how its purpose and scope changed over time.

Mission

The Mission page provides the organization’s substantive statement of purpose. It explains why Redrafting America believes existing systems are struggling; defines its core mission, foundational principles, and values; addresses human rights, liberty, digital democracy, anti-corruption systems, technology, civic responsibility, and long-term goals; and states what the project is not. It also explains the current status of Constitution v2.0. Why it matters: this is the clearest account of what the organization is trying to accomplish and the principles it intends to follow.

Bylaws

The Bylaws section publishes the complete pending governance document: its Preamble, twenty-four Articles, and Epilogue. The Table of Contents provides a plain-language introduction to each Article, while every Article title opens the full governing text, including its sections, lists, tables, and diagrams. Why it matters: readers can examine the actual rules governing organizational identity, authority, leadership, ethics, finances, technology, emergencies, amendments, mission preservation, and dissolution instead of relying on a summary.

Leadership

The Leadership page identifies Todd McGuckin as Founder and President and provides the professional, military, technical, and humanities background relevant to founding Redrafting America. It distinguishes the person currently representing the organization from the larger leadership structure the organization intends to build. Why it matters: the public should be able to identify who presently exercises authority, understand the experience brought to the work, and see when additional officers or directors are added.

Content outline

Contributors & Advisors

The current outline defines how the site will recognize people who make substantial contributions to research, drafting, review, or specialized advice without holding formal governing authority. It separates contributors from advisors, calls for disclosure of each person’s role and limits, and establishes an attribution standard covering authorship, assistance, artificial intelligence, and review. Why it matters: readers should be able to tell who created or evaluated published work and distinguish expert advice from official decision-making.

Content outline

Governance

The current outline identifies four parts of the future governance record: the Bylaws, the authority reserved to the Board, approved organizational policies, and document-control rules for approval, versioning, and publication. The complete pending Bylaws are already available through the dedicated Bylaws section; the remaining governance records require final public content. Why it matters: an organization advocating stronger institutions should make its own authority, decision rules, and controlled documents visible and understandable.

Content outline

Transparency & Accountability

The current outline establishes the categories intended for the organization’s public accountability record: legal and operational status, funding and financial reporting, conflicts of interest, Board records, corrections, and annual reporting. It describes what the section will disclose but does not yet present the completed reporting record. Why it matters: public trust should rest on verifiable status, decisions, corrections, and financial information rather than institutional claims alone.

Content outline

Project Roadmap

The current outline defines the milestones the public roadmap will track: the project’s present stage, the sequence for publishing the Constitution and its Explainers, organizational formation and staffing milestones, partnership and infrastructure work, the future Convention, and the process for controlling changes to the roadmap itself. Why it matters: a dated roadmap will let readers separate completed work from planned work and judge whether the project is advancing as represented.
