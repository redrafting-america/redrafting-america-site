Article XVIII

Mission Preservation Covenants

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article XVIII is an Eternity Clause and protected by the Articles of Incorporation. It is a letter to the future. It names the principles the Nonprofit is built on: truth above all else, the liberty and dignity of every person, democracy that answers to the governed, independence from any political side, and a mission meant to outlast all of us. Whatever the Nonprofit learns, it gives to the public freely whenever it can, and never for private profit. These principles are enduring, difficult to transform, and immutable in the dark. Institutions rarely lose their way all at once. They drift. This is the anchor.

Protected Mission Commitments

The Mission Preservation Covenants protect the Nonprofit’s identity and mission. They define its enduring character and govern every exercise of its authority.

They also govern the interpretation of these Bylaws and advancement of the Nonprofit’s mission. These principles protect the institution for future generations.

No amendment may fundamentally redefine the institution the public has been invited to trust. The Nonprofit shall remain permanently committed to the following principles:

  1. Truth — Veritas Super Omnia (“The Truth Above All Else”); evidence-based reasoning, intellectual honesty, and the continuous pursuit of truth
  2. Human Liberty and Dignity — human dignity; equal rights; and universal human rights
  3. Constitutional Democracy — the rule of law; the principle that legitimate government derives from the informed consent and meaningful participation of the people; the separation, accountability, and responsible exercise of governmental power; peaceful constitutional reform through democratic means; civic education, public empowerment, and the cultivation of informed, engaged, and enthusiastic democratic participation
  4. Institutional Integrity — political neutrality in furtherance of the Nonprofit’s educational mission and the public interest
  5. Mission Permanence — the primary mission of ReDrafting America shall be the peaceful design, continual refinement, public education, and lawful adoption of a modern Constitution for the United States of America founded upon the principles of truth, liberty, accountability, democratic governance, and the long-term flourishing of the American people

Covenant Amendment Safeguards

The Mission Preservation Covenants protect the Nonprofit’s mission and identity through heightened amendment safeguards. Every provision in this Article is an Eternity Clause.

The Constitutional Review Panel acts as an Other Body (an individual or group other than the Board of Directors vested by these Bylaws with powers otherwise exercisable by the Board of Directors or the Member) with binding authority over these amendments. Before Mission Fulfillment Conversion, Complete Governance must exist continuously from proposal through effectiveness.

After conversion, every authorized Mission Fulfillment Board and regular Independent Review Panel position must remain occupied and eligible throughout the process. The reconstituted Constitutional Review Panel then acts under its post-conversion composition and voting requirements.

All participating eligible Constitutional Review Panel members must approve an amendment unanimously. Director members cast one vote each as Panel members and do not vote again in a separate Board proceeding. The serving Steward must also provide affirmative written consent before effectiveness.

The Steward may withdraw consent or veto the amendment before effectiveness. A failed vote, withdrawal, veto, or loss of the applicable governance condition stops the process and requires a new proposal.

The Articles of Incorporation must contain identical Mission Preservation Covenants. An amendment must satisfy both documents’ requirements, and compliance with one process cannot substitute for compliance with the other.

Required Preservation Findings

Before voting, the Constitutional Review Panel must adopt written findings for every proposed amendment to this Article. The findings must explain how the amendment preserves the Mission Preservation Covenants.

The findings must determine each matter below. Each determination must address the proposed amendment as a whole:

  1. Preserves the Nonprofit’s identity and mission
  2. Remains faithful to every Mission Preservation Covenant
  3. Does not fundamentally redefine the Nonprofit’s character, purpose, or institutional integrity
  4. Advances the long-term public interest consistently with the Nonprofit’s Mission

The approved findings become permanent corporate records. The Steward must receive the findings and Constitutional Review Panel voting record before granting written consent.

A material revision invalidates the earlier findings. The Constitutional Review Panel must adopt new findings before voting on the revised amendment.

Covenant Amendment Transparency

Before voting, the Nonprofit must publish the proposed amendment and the Constitutional Review Panel’s adopted preservation findings. Publication provides notice and a public record but grants the public no approval, voting, or decision-making authority.

After voting, the Nonprofit must publish the Panel’s voting record and the Steward’s written consent, withdrawal, or veto. Lawfully protected information may be withheld, but the public record must identify the basis and scope of each withholding.

Invalid Covenant Amendments

An attempted covenant amendment is invalid unless every required safeguard is satisfied. It has no legal force or effect when any requirement is omitted, incomplete, or untimely.

Required safeguards include unanimous approval by all participating eligible Constitutional Review Panel members, adopted preservation findings, public disclosure, and affirmative written Steward consent. They also include the applicable continuous governance condition and compliance with corresponding Articles of Incorporation requirements. A failed attempt must restart as a new proposal.

Steward Authority and Succession

A Steward is an individual entrusted by these Bylaws with protecting and preserving a specified organizational interest. The title confers no general power over the Nonprofit; each Steward’s authority and duties are limited to the applicable provisions.

The Steward assigned to Constitution v2.0 and the Eternity Clauses protects those subjects through the authority expressly granted by these Bylaws. That office provides continuity for mission-critical decisions.

The Member serves as Steward. If no Member serves, the Founder serves; if neither serves, the President serves.

Only one Steward serves at a time, and lower-priority successors hold no concurrent Steward authority. A successor assumes the office automatically when no higher-priority Steward serves.

The Steward must provide affirmative written consent for amendments to Constitution v2.0 and the Eternity Clauses. The Steward may withdraw consent or veto an amendment before effectiveness, but neither action applies retroactively.

A withdrawal or veto stops the pending amendment. Any later attempt must begin as a new proposal and satisfy every applicable safeguard.

An amendment cannot become effective while no eligible Steward serves. A properly installed Acting President may serve when eligible, but an Interim President cannot exercise Steward authority.

Public Benefit Governance

The Nonprofit shall make all knowledge, tools, and platforms it develops freely accessible to the public whenever possible. This commitment applies to every distribution method, including software distributed through digital storefronts or application marketplaces.

Educational content may generate revenue only as needed to sustain operations. It may never generate private profit. All revenue surpluses must support programs that advance civic education and democratic empowerment.

The Nonprofit intentionally follows a Public Benefit Governance Model based on governance principles used by benefit corporations. It considers its Mission, affected communities, long-term public consequences, and transparent reporting when making organizational decisions.

This voluntary model does not change the Nonprofit’s legal classification or convert it into a benefit corporation. It supplements the duties and restrictions imposed by law, the Articles of Incorporation, and these Bylaws.
