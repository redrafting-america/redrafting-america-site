Article XXII

Amendments

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article XXII explains how these Bylaws change. A document that cannot grow will eventually be ignored, and one that can be quietly rewritten was never worth much to begin with. Most of these Bylaws are Standard Clauses, meant to grow with experience. A few are Eternity Clauses, the foundations everything else rests on, and changing them asks far more of everyone involved. Any Director may propose a change, in writing, complete, with an explanation of why it helps. Everyone receives notice and time to think, and every proposal is published before anyone votes. Nobody may stall a proposal instead of voting on it, or take apart sideways what cannot be taken apart directly. Nothing here is beyond change, unless it’s done in the dark.

Bylaw Amendment Proposals

Any Director may submit a written amendment to these Bylaws. The Director must reasonably believe it advances the Mission, improves governance, strengthens legal integrity, or supports long-term effectiveness.

Beginning one year after Minimum Governance is maintained, the Board of Directors shall review these Bylaws on a recurring schedule adopted by Board Resolution. The Secretary shall coordinate each review and record its conclusions. A review does not amend these Bylaws. Any resulting proposal remains subject to this Article and all applicable safeguards.

Every proposal must:

  1. be submitted in writing
  2. identify language proposed for revision, repeal, replacement, or reorganization
  3. provide the complete proposed text
  4. explain the proposal’s purpose, anticipated effects, and supporting rationale

The Secretary shall promptly review each submission for the required components and identify any missing information. This review cannot reject a proposal on its merits, block resubmission, or delay consideration after the proposal becomes complete.

A proposal must comply with the Nonprofit’s Charitable Purposes, Foundational Principles, and applicable federal and Pennsylvania law. Everyone administering the process shall perform assigned duties in good faith and within a reasonable time.

No person or body may improperly delay, obstruct, or refuse a required procedural duty to prevent consideration. These duties do not require approval or limit the Board of Directors’s authority after the required review.

The Board of Directors shall treat amendment as deliberate constitutional development of the Nonprofit’s public governance model. Amendments should preserve stability while responding to experience, improved knowledge, and changing circumstances. This work remains guided by Veritas Super Omnia — “The Truth Above All Else.”

Notice and Public Disclosure

Every proposed Standard Clause amendment shall be delivered to each Director at least fifteen days before the vote. The notice must include the complete proposed text and its required written explanation.

Every proposed Eternity Clause amendment shall be delivered by Confirmed Receipt to each eligible Constitutional Review Panel member and the Steward. Delivery must occur at least thirty days before the vote.

During the applicable notice period, eligible participants may request clarification, suggest revisions, consult experts, and discuss the proposal in good faith. The authorized decision-making body may postpone consideration when additional research, legal review, or constitutional analysis would materially improve its decision.

Before any vote, the Nonprofit shall publish the proposal and its written explanation. An Eternity Clause proposal must also include the Constitutional Review Panel’s adopted preservation findings. Publication provides notice and a permanent public record but grants the public no approval, voting, or decision-making authority.

No amendment may be voted on until every applicable notice and review requirement is satisfied. Postponement may not be used to defeat required consideration through improper delay.

Adoption of Amendments

After all notice and review requirements are satisfied, the authorized decision-making body may vote on the proposal. Each amendment must follow the approval requirements applicable to its clause type.

Every Bylaws provision is a Standard Clause unless it belongs to an Article whose provisions are expressly designated as Eternity Clauses. A Standard Clause follows the ordinary amendment procedure in this Article and requires affirmative votes from three-fourths of Voting Directors.

Absence, unavailability, abstention, recusal, suspension, disqualification, and vacancy affect eligibility, Quorum, and voting under the Board voting rules. No missing response counts as a vote.

Before an Eternity Clause amendment may be adopted, the corresponding protection in the Articles of Incorporation must first be lawfully amended. The proposed Bylaws amendment must then satisfy every heightened safeguard governing the Mission Preservation Covenants, including the applicable continuous governance condition, Constitutional Review Panel approval, adopted findings, public disclosure, and affirmative written Steward consent.

Director members cast one vote each as Constitutional Review Panel members and do not vote again in a separate Board proceeding. No separate Board vote duplicates Constitutional Review Panel approval.

No amendment to a Standard Clause, definition, organizational structure, dependency, or related text may accomplish indirectly what an Eternity Clause safeguard prohibits directly. Classification follows substantive effect rather than drafting form.

No amendment may conflict with applicable law, the Articles of Incorporation, or the Nonprofit’s tax-exempt status. An adopted amendment becomes effective immediately unless its approving action establishes a later effective date.

Publication and Version Control

Every adopted amendment shall be incorporated into the Nonprofit’s official Bylaws. The Board of Directors shall assign the revised Bylaws a unique version identifier and record the adoption and effective dates.

The Secretary shall maintain an official revision history for every adopted version. Each entry must identify the version, adoption date, effective date, and a concise amendment summary.

The current official Bylaws shall be published through the Official Website whenever practicable. The published document must clearly display its official status, version identifier, and publication date.

The Nonprofit shall preserve every prior official version as a permanent organizational record. Archived versions shall remain available for historical, educational, legal, and governance purposes unless applicable law requires restriction.

If published versions conflict, the most recently adopted official version controls. Publication or a later file date cannot replace valid adoption.

The Board of Directors may authorize typographical, grammatical, formatting, citation, numbering, or cross-reference corrections without using the amendment process. A correction must not change legal meaning, intent, interpretation, or substantive effect. The Secretary shall document every correction in the official revision history.
