Article XXI

Emergencies

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article XXI is the plan for the days you hope never arrive. A threat to someone’s safety. The systems locked or stolen. Too few leaders left to govern. A legal crisis. Money that has gone missing. A public storm. The need to act against someone quickly. Something no one foresaw. Or a perfect storm, when several arrive at once. For each, it names who may act, what they may do, and who must be told. People come first, whether a leader, a leader’s family, or a stranger. Three rules control every crisis: emergency power is temporary, actions are reviewed afterward, and no one ever investigates themselves. History is full of institutions that never quite left the emergency. This one has decided, in advance, that every emergency ends. Even in our darkest hour, our enduring principles cannot be set aside.

Personal Safety Emergency

This Section authorizes immediate, temporary action when a credible threat arising from the Nonprofit’s work endangers an Official Representative, their immediate family, or the public. Covered threats include violence, harassment, doxxing, and comparable conduct reasonably believed to endanger physical safety.

Protective action must be necessary, proportionate, and limited to safeguarding people during the emergency. The authority includes protective measures, emergency expenditures, prompt Board notice, and later Board review. Protection does not reduce the affected person’s organizational standing, authority, or responsibilities.

Personal Safety Trigger

A Personal Safety Emergency exists when a credible threat of violence, harassment, doxxing, or comparable conduct reasonably endangers physical safety. The endangered person must be an Official Representative, a member of their immediate family, or a member of the public.

The threat must arise from an Official Representative’s role with the Nonprofit. This requirement applies whether the threat targets that representative, their immediate family, or the public.

Interim Protective Authority

During a Personal Safety Emergency, the President may authorize immediate, reasonable protective measures. Measures may include temporarily withholding identifying information from public materials, engaging security resources, and notifying law enforcement.

If the President is unavailable, conflicted, or endangered, authority passes through the Board Continuity Emergency order of succession. Each unavailable, conflicted, or endangered Director is skipped. If no successor can act, the Chain of Authority supplies the first available conflict-free decision-maker.

Protective Action Notice

The person exercising protective authority shall notify the Board of Directors as soon as reasonably practicable. Notice must describe the threat, protective measures, and basis for acting.

Notice may be delayed when necessary to protect the affected person or avoid interfering with law enforcement. A written summary must reach the Board of Directors within five business days. It may omit protected details when disclosure would endanger safety or impede law enforcement.

Any omission must identify its reason and general scope. The complete information shall be provided when the protective need ends.

Emergency Expenditure Authority

Reasonable expenditures needed to carry out authorized protective measures may be made despite the current annual budget. Spending must remain limited to measures reasonably necessary for the Personal Safety Emergency.

The acting person shall report every expenditure promptly to the Treasurer. The Board of Directors must review and ratify the expenditures at its next regularly convened meeting.

Preservation of Status and Authority

Protective measures do not reduce the affected person’s organizational authority, standing, or responsibilities. Temporarily withholding public identification does not change that rule.

Neither the emergency nor any protective measure may justify adverse treatment of the affected person. Any restriction must remain limited to the protection authorized by this Section.

Emergency Access and Custody

This Section establishes the Nonprofit’s program for preserving lawful control of essential systems, records, communications, and operations during an Emergency Access Event. It governs emergency credentials, recovery mechanisms, and the people entrusted with them.

The program separates ordinary administration from emergency custody and requires independently usable recovery paths. Every activation, test, transfer, and material change remains documented, reviewed, and subject to Board oversight.

Emergency Access Principles

This Subsection governs emergency access to Essential Systems during an Emergency Access Event. An event includes a cyberattack, administrative lockout, technological failure, malicious insider, loss or incapacity of essential personnel, or a comparable continuity threat.

Emergency access exists only to preserve lawful organizational control and restore essential operations. The program shall follow these principles:

  1. Board oversight and ultimate authority
  2. separation of ordinary administration from complete recovery capability
  3. at least two independently usable mechanisms under separate custody
  4. use limited to threat containment, evidence preservation, and restoration of lawful control
  5. documentation and review of every activation, test, and material change
  6. Board knowledge of the offices entrusted with emergency responsibility
  7. credential disclosure limited to people with a legitimate need to know

Emergency Access Terms

Four terms govern emergency access under this Section. They distinguish protected materials, essential resources, recovery mechanisms, and operational independence.

TermDefinition
Emergency MaterialsAuthentication devices, passwords, recovery codes, and related materials needed for emergency access
Essential SystemA technology, communications, financial, identity, or records resource whose loss or compromise could materially impair the Nonprofit
Emergency MechanismAn emergency account or recovery path capable of restoring lawful control of one or more Essential Systems
Independently UsableAble to perform its recovery function without the other Emergency Mechanism or the other Custodian’s Emergency Materials

Each definition applies throughout the emergency access program. The Confidential Emergency Access Policy may add technical detail without changing these meanings.

Emergency Credential Controls

Each Emergency Mechanism belongs to the Nonprofit and must remain separate from personal accounts and devices. Each uses phishing-resistant authentication, has no routine operational purpose, and undergoes required monitoring and testing.

Custody follows the safeguards below. No person may control both complete recovery paths except during the authorized Formation Governance transition.

  1. Divided custody. Each Custodian controls one Independently Usable Emergency Mechanism and may not possess complete credentials for both mechanisms.
  2. Redundancy. Primary and backup materials remain in separate custody and failure domains so one event cannot eliminate recovery capability.
  3. Qualifications. Each Custodian must exercise sound fiduciary judgment, protect confidential property, disclose conflicts, complete training, and accept custody in writing.
  4. Disqualification. The President, Founding Director, Director of Technology and Digital Governance, principal systems administrators, and materially conflicted people cannot serve as Custodians.
  5. Replacement. A disqualified Accountable Custodian is replaced through the Chain of Authority, excluding the Independent Custodian and anyone holding the other Emergency Mechanism. The Board of Directors shall designate a replacement within thirty days and record the appointment. The disqualified person cannot exercise, direct, or advise on that custody.
  6. Formation Governance transition. During Formation Governance, the Founding Director may establish, configure, and temporarily possess both mechanisms’ Emergency Materials. This authority is limited to formation, testing, and continuity and retains all monitoring and reporting requirements. Transfer occurs within thirty days after each Custodian can accept custody or the formation period ends, whichever is later. A documented impediment reported to the Board of Directors may delay transfer.

Emergency Access Activation

Activation ordinarily requires documented approval from two authorized people, including at least one Custodian. Authorized people are both Custodians, the Vice President, a Board-designated Disinterested Director, and a Disinterested President.

Anyone under investigation, suspected of compromise, or materially conflicted cannot authorize or direct activation. If delay risks imminent material harm, either Custodian may independently activate only that Custodian’s Emergency Mechanism.

Independent activation is limited to containment, evidence preservation, and restoration of lawful control. The Custodian must notify the other Custodian, Vice President, Secretary, and available Disinterested Directors as soon as safely practicable.

The Custodian must create a contemporaneous record and submit the activation for prompt Board review. Emergency authority may restore access, contain threats, preserve evidence, and resume essential operations.

Emergency authority cannot support ordinary administration, personal advantage, retaliation, concealment, or interference with a lawful investigation. Emergency privileges end, and exposed credentials must rotate, when secure ordinary administration returns.

Emergency Access Records and Review

Every activation, attempted activation, test, transfer, and material configuration change must be logged under the Confidential Emergency Access Policy. After activation, the responsible Custodian shall notify available Disinterested Directors as soon as practicable.

The Custodian shall provide a written incident report within five business days. It identifies the reason, authorizers, participants, affected systems, actions, preserved evidence, and recommended corrections.

The Board of Directors shall promptly conduct or commission an after-action review through the Chain of Authority. Internal reporting does not replace applicable public breach notification, and one event may require both.

Credentials, recovery codes, storage locations, and technical instructions remain limited to people with a legitimate need to know. Confidentiality cannot conceal authority, evade Board oversight, or obstruct an audit.

A Custodian conducting or directing an investigation cannot hold or access a mechanism or Emergency Materials bearing on that investigation. The Board of Directors shall use the Chain of Authority to appoint a qualified, Disinterested alternate who accepts custody in writing.

The alternate assumes only the affected mechanism and materials, while the other Custodian retains only the other mechanism. If no alternate is available, the affected mechanism and materials remain secured and unused pending appointment.

Every transfer must be logged, reported to the Board of Directors and Independent Directors, and completed under the Confidential Emergency Access Policy. After the investigation, custody returns through a documented handback to a qualified, Disinterested Custodian or successor.

No person may investigate a matter while controlling evidence or Emergency Materials bearing on that investigation. This separation remains mandatory throughout the review.

Emergency Access Readiness

Each Emergency Mechanism must be tested at least once every ninety days. Additional testing follows a Custodian change, material system migration, suspected compromise, or another material event designated by the Board of Directors.

Mechanisms must be tested separately so one remains unaltered and available. Each Custodian shall notify the Board of Directors of an anticipated resignation, incapacity, or inability to continue.

The Board of Directors shall appoint an interim successor promptly, while the vacant mechanism remains secured. A Disinterested Director must witness each transfer and inventory the transferred materials.

Every transfer requires credential rotation and a validation test. Unauthorized possession, disclosure, or misuse of Emergency Materials is a serious duty violation.

The violation may result in access suspension, investigation, removal, or other lawful action. These consequences do not limit any additional remedy available under the Bylaws or law.

Emergency Access Policy

The Board of Directors shall maintain a Confidential Emergency Access Policy implementing this Section. The Policy covers credentials, authentication, activation, system inventories, monitoring, testing, evidence preservation, and incident reporting.

The Board of Directors may amend the Policy without amending these Bylaws. No amendment may conflict with or weaken this Section.

The Nonprofit may maintain a separate Technical Recovery Runbook containing account names, technical instructions, and recovery commands. Access to the Runbook remains controlled under the Policy.

An Emergency Access Event and a Board Continuity Emergency are distinct and may occur separately, together, or neither. The Board of Directors shall review this Section and Policy annually and after every material Emergency Access Event.

Board Continuity Emergency

This Section governs continued operations when a qualifying catastrophe prevents the Board of Directors from readily assembling Quorum. It establishes temporary meeting, authority, succession, and restoration procedures.

While a serving Member can lawfully act, Pillar vacancies follow the Member’s appointment procedure. Independent Director vacancies follow their separate appointment procedure.

Emergency succession, custody, and reconstitution apply only when neither the Board of Directors nor Member can act. Reviewers under the Chain of Authority must certify terminated membership, Permanent Incapacity, or suspended appointment powers before using these procedures.

Continuity Purpose and Legal Basis

A Board Continuity Emergency preserves lawful governance when a qualifying catastrophe prevents ordinary Board Quorum from readily assembling. It uses temporary procedures authorized for nonprofit boards by 15 Pa.C.S. § 5509.

A Board Continuity Emergency is distinct from an Emergency Access Event. Either may arise alone, together, or not at all.

Board Continuity Trigger

A Board Continuity Emergency exists when a qualifying catastrophe prevents the Board of Directors from readily assembling Quorum. Qualifying causes include disaster, epidemic, pandemic, attack, government-declared emergency, or a comparable event substantially impairing ordinary meetings.

Any Director who reasonably believes the condition exists may declare the emergency. The Director must promptly notify every reasonably reachable Director and eligible reviewers under the Chain of Authority.

Those reviewers must confirm or reject the condition within seventy-two hours after notification. Rejection or failure to confirm ends the declaration automatically and restores ordinary governance.

The Board of Directors shall ratify or reconsider a confirmed declaration at the earliest practicable opportunity. Good-faith actions remain valid despite later reconsideration.

The Independent Review Panel, acting through eligible Disinterested members, or a competent court may find that an action lacked good faith or a reasonable basis. That action is voidable and receives no protection under this provision.

Emergency Meetings and Notice

During a Board Continuity Emergency, notice may use any method reasonably calculated to reach Directors. Public posting or broadcast may be used, and notice may be shortened when advance notice is impracticable.

Meetings may occur entirely through electronic means when ordinary participation requirements cannot practicably be satisfied. Emergency meetings should be recorded when safety and circumstances permit, and all other applicable emergency-meeting safeguards remain in force.

Reduced Emergency Quorum

When the Eligible Seats cannot readily assemble, available and participating Directors constitute Quorum during the Board Continuity Emergency. At least three Directors must participate, and reduced Quorum does not expand anyone’s voting eligibility.

Reduced Quorum may preserve corporate existence, records, property, insurance, legal compliance, and essential operations. It may also address matters that cannot reasonably await restoration of ordinary Quorum.

Reduced Quorum cannot authorize a major transaction, terminate personnel, fill a Pillar Director position, approve relocation, or suspend or remove a Director. It cannot take any action requiring more than an ordinary majority.

Whenever fewer than seven qualified, onboarded positions remain occupied before conversion, the Minimum Governance restrictions apply. The stricter applicable emergency or lifecycle limitation controls.

Succession Application and Emergency Duration

A Director may hold temporary emergency duty authority over multiple Pillars during a Board Continuity Emergency. Receiving that authority does not appoint an Acting Director, occupy the affected position, or provide another vote or Quorum credit. A separate Acting Director appointment remains available under the Acting Director rules when its requirements are satisfied.

Temporary emergency duty authority ends when the Pillar is filled, its Director returns, a lawful Acting Director appointment takes effect, or the Board Continuity Emergency ends.

The emergency ends immediately when its qualifying condition ends. Otherwise, it expires thirty calendar days after confirmation unless renewed before that period ends.

Each renewal lasts no more than thirty calendar days. It requires a written Board finding that ordinary Quorum still cannot readily assemble.

The Board of Directors must approve renewal under the emergency procedures. Eligible reviewers under the Chain of Authority must independently confirm the finding.

Successive renewals may continue only while the same qualifying condition persists. Every renewal remains subject to the same findings, approval, and independent confirmation.

Total Board Unavailability

When every incumbent Director is temporarily incapacitated or unavailable, each Director retains their seat. The Independent Review Panel may take only actions reasonably necessary to preserve records, assets, legal standing, and lawful access.

The Independent Review Panel cannot act as a Director, vote, count toward Quorum, appoint or remove Directors, amend governing documents, or exercise program authority. Its preservation authority ends when an authorized incumbent Director can resume lawful action.

The Independent Review Panel shall record every preservation action and return control of affected records and property. Emergency credentials remain subject to the emergency access and custody requirements.

Catastrophic Board Loss

A Catastrophic Board Loss exists only when every Director position is vacant and no individual holds Director office. Temporary incapacity or unavailability does not create this condition.

The first available, qualified, Disinterested level in the Chain of Authority must verify and certify the loss in writing. Restoration cannot begin before certification, but preservation duties activate immediately when the condition exists.

Before Mission Fulfillment Conversion, Catastrophic Board Loss returns the Nonprofit to Formation Governance as an express exception to ordinary lifecycle non-regression. After conversion, restoration remains within Legacy Governance, may fill only authorized Mission Fulfillment Board positions, and cannot reopen a permanently closed Pillar position.

Catastrophic Preservation Custody

The Independent Custodian shall preserve the Nonprofit’s records, assets, legal standing, insurance, and lawful access until governance is restored. Preservation authority does not confer Director, Member, program, or general governance authority, and obligations must remain limited to reasonable preservation needs.

Founder Recall

Catastrophic Board Loss automatically activates Founder recall. The Nonprofit’s serving legal representative has one week after certification to give the living Founder notice by Confirmed Receipt. The Founder then has one week after receipt to accept through a signed Board Resolution filed in the corporate record.

The acceptance resolution becomes effective as the restoration record without approval by a nonexistent Board. An eligible Founder who was never removed for Just Cause returns exactly as Founder and Member and, as Founder, occupies the Founding Director position. Formation Governance then operates under its ordinary powers, responsibilities, limitations, and onboarding requirements.

A written decline or failure to file the acceptance resolution by the deadline ends the current recall attempt and immediately activates the Designated Successor pathway. It does not by itself erase any separate future personal return right.

Designated Successor and Designated Alternate

The Designated Successor receives one week after Confirmed Receipt to accept the Member position through the required written restoration Board Resolution. A written decline or missed deadline advances immediately to the Designated Alternate, who receives the same one-week period. A failure at that stage advances immediately to the Chain of Authority.

The accepted restoration resolution becomes effective without a preexisting Board vote. The new Member receives only the reserved Member powers and no Founder, Founder Emeritus, Founding Director, Director, Corporate Officer, or protected-tenure status merely through succession.

The Member’s first restoration responsibility is ensuring that qualified people occupy the President, Secretary, and Treasurer positions. Qualified incumbents remain in office. The Member may appoint the Designated Alternate as President if qualified and accepted. If a serving President becomes Member, the presidency becomes vacant, while qualified Secretary and Treasurer incumbents remain.

A person appointed to office uses that office’s title; the underlying Designated Alternate status remains dormant. If that person later succeeds as Member, acceptance automatically ends the presidency, and the new Member must fill the presidency and every independently vacant Big 3 position.

Chain Selection of a Member

If neither designee can serve, the Chain of Authority stops at the first level able to act lawfully. That level shall certify why prior pathways failed, identify and vet an eligible candidate, obtain signed acceptance and conflict disclosures, appoint the candidate as Member through a written determination preserved in the corporate record, and publish notice of restored membership subject to lawful privacy and security protections.

During Catastrophic Board Loss, both Director levels ordinarily are vacant, so the first available level may be the Disinterested Independent Review Panel, followed by Disinterested Independent Counsel and then the courts. The selected Member receives no Founder or Director status and remains responsible for establishing the Big 3.

Interim President

An Interim President temporarily holds the presidency while the organization identifies and appoints a permanent President. The Board, Member, or another person or body expressly authorized by these Bylaws may make the appointment through a written resolution stating the authority, duties, restrictions, and transition period. The office ends automatically when the permanent President takes office.

A former Founder or Member removed for Just Cause is disqualified from every ordinary return, recall, designation, appointment, and succession pathway. That person may be recalled only as the final measure of last resort after the Designated Successor, Designated Alternate, every other qualified candidate, and all five Chain levels have been exhausted.

The court must certify in writing that it cannot or will not provide a replacement Board. That certification automatically activates the last-resort recall. The recalled person has one week after Confirmed Receipt to accept through a written restoration resolution and serves only as Interim President.

Last-resort service does not reverse or erase the Just Cause finding and confers no Member, Founder, Founder Emeritus, Founding Director, protected-tenure, Steward, or continuing governance status. The Interim President may preserve the organization and exercise only the temporary appointment authority necessary to establish the President, Secretary, and Treasurer positions and restore Minimum Governance.

When a permanent President takes office and Minimum Governance is restored, the Interim President’s office and authority end automatically. The restored Board then proceeds under ordinary governance without that person’s continuing control.

Restoration Completion and Records

Before conversion, the renewed Formation Governance period ends when Minimum Governance is attained. After conversion, restoration ends when the authorized Mission Fulfillment Board can operate under the applicable Legacy Governance rules. Catastrophic Board Loss alone does not dissolve the Nonprofit.

The restored Secretary shall preserve the certification, notices, resolutions, acceptances, eligibility checks, deadlines, custody actions, Chain determinations, appointments, and final outcome. At the first regularly convened meeting, the restored Board shall review emergency operational actions and correct any action exceeding the authority that existed when taken.

Legal and Regulatory Emergency

This Section governs urgent legal and regulatory demands that cannot await ordinary Board action. It authorizes narrow interim measures to preserve rights and meet deadlines.

The responsible Official Representative must notify leadership promptly and preserve every record covered by a litigation hold. Board authorization remains required for admissions, settlements, and discretionary commitments.

Legal and Regulatory Trigger

A Legal and Regulatory Emergency arises when legal process imposes a requirement that cannot reasonably await the next regular Board meeting. The process may include a subpoena, court order, injunction, urgent law-enforcement request, or regulatory investigation.

The emergency exists only while prompt action remains necessary to meet the requirement or protect the Nonprofit’s rights. Ordinary Board authority resumes when the urgent need ends.

Interim Legal Authority

The Director of Legal Affairs and Compliance (CLO) may retain legal counsel, issue a litigation hold, and take necessary protective legal action. Each measure must comply with legal process or preserve the Nonprofit’s rights while awaiting Board authorization.

When the CLO is implicated, conflicted, or unavailable, the Chain of Authority assigns this authority to the first available conflict-free body. That body may arrange qualified legal services under the approved legal-services procedure.

Legal Emergency Notice

The acting person shall notify the President (CEO) and Secretary as soon as reasonably practicable. The notice must identify the legal process and any immediate deadline.

Within five business days, the acting person shall provide the Board of Directors a written summary. It must describe the action taken, its basis, and any continuing obligation.

Limits on Interim Legal Authority

Interim authority is limited to protective and preservative measures. It does not authorize admitting liability, entering a binding settlement, or making a discretionary commitment without Board authorization.

The Board of Directors retains authority to commence, defend, prosecute, compromise, or settle legal proceedings. A legally compelled commitment may be made only to the extent required by law.

Litigation Hold Compliance

Every Official Representative and Custodian shall preserve records and communications within an issued litigation hold’s scope. The hold overrides every ordinary retention or deletion schedule while it remains effective.

Preservation must follow the Technology and Records Standards and any instructions accompanying the hold. No Covered Person may alter, conceal, destroy, or permit deletion of protected information.

Financial Emergency

This Section governs credible threats of financial harm, misconduct, or lost control over the Nonprofit’s assets. It authorizes immediate protective action while preserving fair investigation procedures.

A financial suspension protects assets without deciding misconduct. Authorized financial access must return promptly when the investigation does not substantiate the suspected threat.

Financial Emergency Trigger

A Financial Emergency arises from reasonable suspicion that an Official Representative misappropriated, diverted, or misused Nonprofit funds or financial access. It also arises when a financial account or instrument may be compromised.

This emergency concerns suspected misuse or compromise beyond an Emergency Access Event. Either condition authorizes the protective procedures established in this Section.

Emergency Financial Suspension

The President (CEO) and Director of Ethics and Oversight (CIO), acting jointly, may suspend the suspected person’s financial authority immediately. The suspension may cover signatory, wire, ACH, electronic banking, and comparable financial credentials.

They may direct the Treasurer (CFO) to notify affected financial institutions. The Vice President acts when the President (CEO) is implicated, conflicted, or unavailable.

When the CIO cannot act, the Chain of Authority assigns that role to the first available conflict-free body. The same hierarchy applies when another required participant cannot act without a conflict.

No person who declares the emergency or authorizes the suspension may direct its investigation. A conflict-free investigator shall conduct the review under the applicable investigation procedures.

Financial Emergency Notice

The acting persons shall notify the Board of Directors promptly after imposing a financial suspension. The initial notice must identify the affected authority and immediate protective action.

Within five business days, they shall provide a written summary of the suspected misuse. The summary must explain the action taken and its factual basis.

Protective Financial Suspension

A financial suspension is a protective interim measure. It does not establish misconduct, breach of fiduciary duty, or Just Cause.

The suspension is not disciplinary action and cannot be recorded or characterized as discipline. That characterization may occur only after the applicable investigation and disciplinary procedures substantiate it.

Financial Investigation and Restoration

Every financial suspension shall proceed promptly to a conflict-free investigation under the applicable procedures. Any resulting Director discipline must follow the established removal and accountability requirements.

Suspended financial authority must be restored promptly when the investigation does not substantiate the suspicion. Restoration is also required when a reasonable investigation period expires without substantiation.

Communications Emergency

This Section governs the Nonprofit’s public response to a declared Communications Emergency. It establishes a single authorized voice and an orderly succession when that spokesperson cannot act.

Every response must protect accuracy, public trust, confidentiality, and the Mission. The Board of Directors reviews the response after the emergency.

Communications Emergency Trigger

A Communications Emergency is a sudden event likely to materially mislead the public about the Nonprofit’s identity, statements, positions, or operations. Examples include impersonated channels, falsely attributed documents, coordinated disinformation, or a rapidly escalating public controversy.

The emergency exists when delay or an uncoordinated response could materially harm the Mission, credibility, or public trust. Ordinary criticism or disagreement alone does not establish this emergency.

Emergency Spokesperson

The Director of Communications and Civic Engagement (CCO) is the authorized spokesperson during a Communications Emergency. If that person cannot act, authority passes through the established succession order.

The order is a designated Deputy Communications Director, the Pillar’s Acting Director, the President (CEO), and the Vice President (DEO). Each successor must be available and conflict-free.

Anyone personally involved in the emergency or whose conduct caused it is conflicted and must recuse. Authority then passes to the next eligible person.

No other Director may state the Nonprofit’s institutional position during the emergency. Other Official Representatives may communicate only as directed by the authorized spokesperson.

Communications Notice and Coordination

The authorized spokesperson shall notify the President (CEO) and Secretary as soon as reasonably practicable. The notice must identify the emergency and the planned public response.

If an official account or system may be compromised or impersonated, the spokesperson shall notify its Accountable Custodian immediately. Within five business days, the spokesperson shall give the Board of Directors a written response summary.

Emergency Communications Standards

Every public response must be truthful, accurate, and consistent with the Foundational Principles. It must correct material inaccuracies as promptly as reasonably possible.

A response must preserve information protected by the Bylaws, applicable law, or an active emergency response. Urgency does not permit speculation, avoidable disclosure, or misleading reassurance.

Communications Response Review

The Board of Directors shall review every Communications Emergency response at its next regularly convened meeting. The review must examine accuracy, timeliness, coordination, confidentiality, and effects on public trust.

The Board of Directors may adopt corrective guidance for future incidents. Any required follow-up shall identify responsibility, deadlines, and the record retained for institutional learning.

Emergency Disciplinary Authority

This Section authorizes narrow, temporary disciplinary action when immediate intervention is necessary. It preserves the Board of Directors’ ultimate disciplinary authority and every applicable due-process protection.

Every action requires a written Emergency Action Order, prompt independent review, and automatic expiration. Special safeguards protect Independent Directors during a Protected Founder Review.

Emergency Discipline Purpose

Emergency Disciplinary Authority protects safety, integrity, lawful operations, assets, public trust, and the Mission during extraordinary circumstances. It permits immediate protection when waiting for ordinary Board action would materially increase substantial harm.

This authority is temporary and protective. The Board of Directors retains ultimate authority over discipline, subject to the independent procedure governing a Protected Founder Review.

Emergency Discipline Standard

The Founding Director may exercise Emergency Disciplinary Authority against one or more Directors. The authority exists only when every required condition is satisfied in reasonable, good-faith judgment.

The following conditions apply to each affected Director individually. A shared incident does not eliminate the need for separate findings.

  1. The conduct presents an immediate and substantial threat to the Nonprofit, its Mission, people, assets, information, legal compliance, or public trust.
  2. Delaying action until the Board of Directors can reasonably convene would materially increase the threat.
  3. Immediate intervention is necessary to prevent or mitigate substantial harm.

The authority may address several Directors simultaneously when each meets these standards. Each action remains separately reviewable under this Section.

Succession of Emergency Discipline Authority

Emergency Disciplinary Authority follows the constitutional succession established by the Bylaws. The highest-ranking eligible person holds it, except during the independent procedure governing a Protected Founder Review.

While serving as a Director, the Founding Director holds this authority. During temporary absence, incapacity, recusal, suspension, or other inability, authority passes to the President (CEO) or Acting President.

Upon the Founding Director’s permanent cessation of Board service, authority vests permanently in the office of President (CEO). No separate vote, appointment, assignment, delegation, or amendment is required for either succession.

Only one individual may exercise the authority at a time, except when the Independent Review Panel acts collectively under the protected-review procedure. The authority cannot be delegated outside the established succession.

Emergency Disciplinary Authority cannot permanently remove a Director or diminish the Board of Directors’s ultimate authority. It must be exercised sparingly, reasonably, in good faith, and only as necessary pending review.

Authorized Emergency Measures

The Founding Director may temporarily suspend an affected Director from office. The Founding Director may also restrict organizational authority as reasonably necessary to protect the Nonprofit.

Restrictions may cover facilities, systems, records, financial accounts, confidential information, assets, and other organizational resources. Each restriction must address the existing emergency and remain no broader than reasonably necessary.

Emergency Discipline Examples

Emergency circumstances may involve threats to people, information, finances, property, legal compliance, operations, reputation, or the Mission. The examples below illustrate the standard without limiting it.

Each example must still present an immediate and substantial threat that cannot reasonably await Board action. The underlying facts must satisfy the governing standard for every affected Director.

  1. a credible threat to an Official Representative or member of the public;
  2. unauthorized disclosure, theft, destruction, or compromise of confidential information, records, or cybersecurity systems;
  3. credible evidence of fraud, embezzlement, theft, or comparable financial misconduct;
  4. intentional destruction, misuse, or unlawful diversion of organizational assets;
  5. conduct creating immediate and substantial legal, regulatory, operational, or reputational harm; or
  6. another immediate and substantial threat that cannot reasonably await the Board of Directors.

Emergency Action Order

Immediately after exercising Emergency Disciplinary Authority, the authorized person or body shall prepare a written Emergency Action Order. The issuer must sign and date the Order.

The Order must state each required element clearly. Its record must permit meaningful review of the action and its necessity.

  1. the facts creating the emergency;
  2. the specific action taken;
  3. the reasons immediate intervention was necessary;
  4. the effective date and time; and
  5. every temporary restriction imposed.

The issuer shall deliver the Order to every Director as soon as reasonably practicable. Any verifiable official communication method authorized by the Bylaws, law, or Board Resolution satisfies delivery.

Delivery constitutes official notice for the required review. The Secretary shall preserve evidence of when and how delivery occurred.

Emergency Action Review

The authorized reviewing body shall convene as soon as reasonably practicable and within fourteen calendar days after delivery. Every affected Director is excluded from participation, Quorum, and voting-threshold calculations.

Other conflicted, suspended, or ineligible Directors are also excluded. Each affected Director retains applicable notice, response, evidentiary, and due-process rights.

Review follows the Chain of Authority and applicable participation requirements. When no authorized Director body can act, the Independent Review Panel shall complete review within the same fourteen-day period.

At least four eligible, Disinterested Independent Review Panel members must participate, and three affirmative votes are required. Vacancies or a failed vote alone do not transfer authority.

An Order involving a protected Independent Director during a Protected Founder Review follows its separate procedure. That procedure retains its seven-day period and unanimity requirement.

Before deciding, the reviewing body shall allow each affected Director to be heard, present evidence, answer allegations, and exercise applicable due-process rights. It shall determine whether the emergency standard and imposed measures were justified.

The reviewing body may affirm, modify, or reverse the Order in whole or part. Its determination is final unless the Bylaws or applicable law provide otherwise.

Reversal restores the affected Director retroactively to the suspension’s effective date. Restoration includes compensation and benefits lost solely because of the reversed measures.

Independent Director Protection During Founder Review

During a Protected Founder Review, the Founding Director, any Pillar Director, the President (CEO), and any Acting President cannot act against a protected Independent Director. Prohibited actions include suspension, removal, disqualification, and Emergency Disciplinary Authority.

A protected Independent Director is serving or eligible to serve as a Disinterested reviewer in that matter. Protected participation, evidence, deliberation, recommendations, and votes cannot support adverse action.

A Protected Founder Review begins when a Director documents a good-faith allegation potentially supporting removal for Just Cause or Permanent Incapacity. It ends only upon final disposition or documented closure.

An unrelated urgent allegation against a protected Independent Director shall be referred to the Independent Review Panel. The allegation must independently satisfy the emergency-discipline standard.

The Independent Review Panel alone may investigate and issue or decline an Emergency Action Order in that circumstance. At least four eligible, Disinterested members must participate, and issuance requires unanimity.

The Independent Review Panel may appoint one qualified, Disinterested external person as a temporary reviewer while the affected Independent Director cannot participate. The appointee gains no Board position, vote, or Quorum status, and service ends with the need.

The Order remains subject to applicable notice, written-order, response, records, and expiration safeguards. Within seven calendar days after delivery, the Independent Review Panel shall consider the response and available evidence.

At least four eligible, Disinterested members must participate, and they must unanimously affirm, modify, or reverse the Order in writing. Otherwise, the Order expires automatically, and every such Order expires within thirty calendar days.

The Founder, every Pillar Director, and every other interested person shall have no role in the determination. Protected participation or voting cannot be characterized as misconduct.

Emergency Order Expiration

An Emergency Action Order expires automatically unless the reviewing body affirms or modifies it within the applicable review period. Every measure resting solely on the Order expires with it.

Every Order expires within thirty calendar days after becoming effective, even when affirmed or modified. Continued restrictions require separate authorization through the applicable ordinary disciplinary procedure.

Emergency Discipline Records

The Secretary shall preserve every Emergency Action Order, supporting document, and notice provided to the Board of Directors. The permanent record must also include related meeting records and the final determination.

The Secretary shall maintain these materials under the Technology and Records Standards. Confidentiality protections apply without reducing the completeness or integrity of the record.

General Emergency Authority

This Section provides limited interim authority for a genuine emergency outside every specifically defined emergency type. It cannot replace a more specific procedure that applies.

Authorized action remains protective, temporary, and subject to prompt notice and Board review. The Board of Directors may ratify, modify, or unwind the action.

General Emergency Trigger

A General Emergency is a sudden, material threat to the Nonprofit’s safety, assets, operations, or Mission requiring immediate action. The threat cannot reasonably await the next regular Board meeting.

This residual authority applies only when no other emergency type governs the event. A more specific emergency procedure controls whenever its trigger is satisfied.

General Emergency Action

The President (CEO) and Director of Ethics and Oversight (CIO), acting jointly, may take immediately necessary protective action. They may secure property, engage needed professionals, and direct Official Representatives while awaiting Board authorization.

The Vice President (DEO) acts when the President (CEO) is implicated, conflicted, or unavailable. When another required participant cannot act, the Chain of Authority supplies the first available conflict-free replacement.

General Emergency Notice

The acting persons shall notify the Board of Directors as soon as reasonably practicable. The initial notice must identify the emergency and immediate protective measures.

Within five business days, they shall provide a written summary of the emergency. The summary must explain every action taken and its factual basis.

Limits on General Emergency Action

General emergency action is limited to protective measures reasonably necessary to address the immediate threat. Its scope and duration cannot exceed the emergency’s demonstrated needs.

This authority cannot permit any action reserved exclusively to the Board of Directors. It also cannot avoid a limit, safeguard, or approval required elsewhere in the Bylaws.

General Emergency Review

The Board of Directors shall review every general emergency action at its next regularly convened meeting. It may ratify, modify, or direct the action’s orderly unwinding.

Unratified authority expires thirty days after the Board of Directors first receives notice. Expiration does not invalidate a protective measure lawfully completed before that deadline.

Emergency Coordination

This Section explains how the emergency authorities in this Article operate together. Each emergency type remains independent, although one event may activate several types.

Concurrent procedures must be coordinated without weakening their separate safeguards. The Board of Directors resolves any direct conflict consistently with the Mission and fiduciary duties.

Independent Emergency Types

Each emergency type in this Article has its own trigger, authority, safeguards, and duration. A determination under one type does not automatically establish another.

A single event may create one emergency type, several types, or none. Each asserted emergency must independently satisfy its governing requirements.

Concurrent Emergency Procedures

Several emergency procedures may apply concurrently to the same event. Each procedure remains governed by its own authority, duties, safeguards, deadlines, and records requirements.

One procedure does not limit another unless the Bylaws expressly provide otherwise. Concurrent use cannot expand any participant’s authority beyond the combined procedures’ lawful scope.

Coordination of Emergency Responses

People acting under concurrent emergency procedures shall coordinate their work reasonably. They must share information necessary for an effective, accurate, and consistent response.

Coordination remains subject to each procedure’s confidentiality and access restrictions. Information may be shared only with people authorized to receive it.

Default Emergency Review and Closure

A specific review, expiration, or closure rule controls whenever one applies. Otherwise, the Board of Directors reviews emergency action at its next regular meeting.

The Board of Directors may ratify, modify, or terminate the action. The emergency ends immediately when its triggering condition no longer exists.

Temporary measures end with the emergency unless law, contract, or separate authority requires continuation. The responsible person shall document closure, completed actions, and every remaining obligation.

The closure record shall be delivered to the Secretary. The Secretary shall preserve it under the Technology and Records Standards.

Protection of Mission Preservation Covenants

No emergency authority may suspend, limit, override, or amend the Mission Preservation Covenants. Every Eternity Clause remains fully effective throughout an emergency.

Emergency action must preserve the heightened safeguards governing those provisions. Urgency cannot substitute for any required approval, finding, disclosure, or Steward consent.

Conflicts Between Emergency Procedures

The Board of Directors shall resolve any direct conflict between emergency procedures as promptly as circumstances reasonably permit. It shall act through a recorded Board Resolution.

The resolution must preserve this Article’s purposes, the Mission, and every applicable fiduciary duty. It cannot enlarge emergency authority or waive a mandatory safeguard.
