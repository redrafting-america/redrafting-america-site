Epilogue

The closing reflection and invitation in the pending Bylaws of Redrafting America.

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

E Pluribus Unum asked how thirteen states could become one.

Ex Uno Floremus asks what that one nation becomes.

Veritas Super Omnia is how we answer.

These Bylaws have spent their formidable length answering both questions in the language they require throughout. That means triggers and thresholds, funds and filings, offices and obstacles. That precision was necessary. A plan this consequential cannot survive on good intentions alone. It is also, by itself, not enough.

No constitution in the history of the world has ever governed a single day on its own. Not the one written in Philadelphia in 1787. And not whatever version emerges from Constitution v2.0, advances through an Article V convention, and is ratified by the States. A constitution is only paper and principle: a plan for who holds power, how that power is checked, and what it may never reach. Whether the plan holds is decided afterward by the Citizens who show up. They vote, serve, sit on juries, run for office, stay informed, and take part in the Democracy they chose. In other words, they pay attention, year after year, long after everyone who drafted it has left the room.

ReDrafting America was founded on one conviction, stated plainly in the Preamble: Each generation is responsible for leaving the Republic stronger than it inherited. Everything written between that Preamble and this sentence exists to make that conviction operable, not merely felt. That includes the research, the safeguards, and the independent review built into every stage. But an institution can only build the scaffolding. It cannot live the citizenship for anyone. That part was never ReDrafting America’s to perform. It was always the Citizen’s.

So take these closing words as an invitation, not an instruction. It is extended equally to the Director bound by every other page of these Bylaws and to the Citizen encountering them for the first time.

Participate.

Read the constitutional drafts as they are published. Argue the details in the Digital Constitutional Convention. Vote as though your future depends on it, because it does. A Republic conceived as a light for others to steer by does not stay lit on its own. It takes people willing to be the light themselves, in ordinary rooms, on ordinary days, long after the drafting is done.

Constitution v2.0 may advance through an Article V convention and be ratified by the States. It may instead be set aside for a better draft these Bylaws never anticipated. Whatever becomes of it, the obligation described here does not expire with it. Ex Uno Floremus was never a prediction. It is a standing instruction, addressed to whoever is reading it, in whatever year they read it…

Not because a document commands it. But because you decide, again and again, to make it true.

From One, We Flourish.
