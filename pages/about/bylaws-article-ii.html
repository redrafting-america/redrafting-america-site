Article II

Legal Structure

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article II is the ground the whole enterprise stands on. It says what the Nonprofit is in the eyes of the law and which document wins when two disagree. One Member holds a deliberately narrow power. Everyone doing the work answers to the mission, not to any person, donor, or faction. When authority is unclear, a Chain of Authority decides who acts. There is never a moment when no one is responsible. The records are kept not for years, but for generations. No one admires a foundation. Everyone stands on one.

Principal Office

The principal office of ReDrafting America shall be located within the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. Its location shall be designated by the Board of Directors from time to time.

The Board of Directors may establish, relocate, consolidate, discontinue, or designate additional physical or virtual offices, facilities, mailing addresses, or other places of business. It may do so within or outside the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. It may do so whenever it determines that doing so will further the Charitable Purposes, educational mission, or efficient administration of the Nonprofit.

The designation or relocation of any office shall not require an amendment to these Bylaws unless otherwise required by applicable law.

Corporate Status

ReDrafting America is organized as a nonprofit corporation under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. It shall operate exclusively for charitable, educational, scientific, and other purposes permitted under Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(3). Any corresponding provision of future federal tax law applies equally.

The Nonprofit shall maintain its corporate existence and legal status in good standing. It shall comply with all applicable federal, state, and local laws governing nonprofit organizations.

Nothing in these Bylaws shall be interpreted to authorize any activity inconsistent with the Nonprofit’s Articles of Incorporation, its federal tax-exempt status, or applicable law.

Governing Authority

These Bylaws are adopted pursuant to the Articles of Incorporation of ReDrafting America. They shall supplement and be interpreted consistently with the Articles of Incorporation and the Pennsylvania Nonprofit Corporation Law of 1988, as amended. They shall likewise be interpreted consistently with the Internal Revenue Code and all other applicable federal, state, and local laws. In the event of any conflict, applicable law and the Articles of Incorporation shall control.

A court of competent jurisdiction, or the operation of applicable law, may determine that a provision of these Bylaws is invalid, illegal, or unenforceable. If so, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect. The invalid provision shall be interpreted, reformed, or replaced to the minimum extent necessary to achieve compliance with applicable law. In doing so, the original intent and purpose of these Bylaws shall be preserved to the greatest extent reasonably possible.

The Board of Directors may discover a conflict with applicable law, the Articles of Incorporation, or the Nonprofit’s federal tax-exempt status. It shall then amend these Bylaws within a reasonable time. In doing so, it shall preserve, to the greatest extent reasonably possible, the original intent, mission, Foundational Principles, and Charitable Purposes of the Nonprofit.

Chain of Authority

The Chain of Authority identifies the decision-maker for a matter expressly assigned to it. It also resolves governance gaps when authority is unknown, unavailable, or impaired by conflict. It does not apply merely because an authorized body disagrees, deadlocks, or rejects a proposal.

An assigned matter proceeds in the following order. It stops at the first level able to act lawfully and satisfy every applicable decision requirement:

  1. Disinterested Pillar Directors
  2. Disinterested Independent Directors
  3. Disinterested Independent Review Panel members
  4. Disinterested Independent Counsel
  5. A court of competent jurisdiction

Each level must satisfy every applicable independence, eligibility, conflict, participation, and approval requirement. Quorum applies when the acting level is a multi-person body. Independent Counsel acts only when qualified, licensed, Disinterested, and able to act lawfully. The court level remains the fifth and final resort.

After Mission Fulfillment Conversion, no Independent Directors serve, so a matter reaching the second level proceeds to the next level able to act.

The Chain of Authority does not create authority, supply missing votes, waive requirements, or transfer powers reserved to another person or body. It stops at the first level able to act lawfully and cannot be used merely to reverse disagreement, deadlock, or rejection by an authorized decision-maker.

Statutory Member

The organization has one Member with a limited role: appointing and removing Pillar Directors and consenting to the most significant changes. All other powers belong to the Board of Directors.

The Member acts only in writing and cannot replace the Directors’ own judgment. The rules below explain who takes over when the Member is absent, how a living Founder returns, and what records are required.

Membership and the Board of Directors

ReDrafting America has one (1) statutory voting member (the “Member”), established by the Articles of Incorporation. The Member holds only the powers reserved by the Articles of Incorporation, subject to their limitations. Every corporate power not expressly reserved to the Member is vested in the Board of Directors.

Membership confers governance rights only. It creates no ownership, proprietary, economic, or beneficial interest in the Nonprofit or in any of its assets. It confers no entitlement to compensation, to employment, or to continued service in any office.

Member Actions

The Member must act through a written document that the Member signs and files with the Secretary. The document must clearly state that the person is acting as the Member. Acting as a Director or officer does not exercise the Member’s powers. Removing a Pillar Director must also follow the Articles of Incorporation’s Member-removal requirements and the Director Removals rules.

The Member acts in writing, not through meetings. Within seven days after adoption, the Secretary must deliver every Board Resolution requiring the Member’s appointment or consent using Confirmed Receipt.

Required Member consent must follow the Articles of Incorporation. It is separate from and additional to the Board of Directors’s vote and must be kept in the official corporate records.

Fiduciary Independence

Directors shall exercise their authority solely as fiduciaries for the Charitable Purposes of the Nonprofit. They shall not act as representatives of the Member, any donor group, political affiliation, special interest, or other external constituency. The Member’s reserved powers concern who is entrusted with governance and the protection of the Nonprofit’s constitutional identity. They do not extend to the ordinary affairs of the Nonprofit.

These provisions shall not be construed to limit or diminish the Nonprofit’s commitment to transparency, civic engagement, public participation, or public accountability. Those commitments stand as these Bylaws otherwise provide.

Member Powers

The Member has these powers:

  1. appointing or declining to appoint Pillar Directors nominated by the Board of Directors
  2. designating a Pillar Director candidate directly when the Board of Directors makes no nomination within ninety days of a vacancy, under the vacancy rules
  3. removing Pillar Directors
  4. consenting to voluntary dissolution, mergers, or sales of substantially all assets
  5. consenting to changes to the Articles of Incorporation provisions governing membership and the Member’s reserved powers

The Member cannot appoint or remove Independent Directors.

Transfers and licenses of intellectual property require Member consent when required by the Articles of Incorporation or these Bylaws.

The Member must follow the requirements governing each power.

Member Continuity

The Articles of Incorporation govern the single membership, successor and alternate choices, and selection by Disinterested Directors when deadlines are missed. They also govern temporary Acting Member service and the Founder’s personal right to reclaim membership.

The successor Member receives all reserved powers. A validly serving Acting Member may give required Member consent within that person’s authority. Constitutional Trustees do not receive these powers.

A successor Member must be someone other than the President and receives no rights reserved personally to the Founder. The President keeps the presidential and Steward powers established by these Bylaws.

Member Conflicts of Interest

The Member shall not exercise any reserved power on matters involving the Member’s personal interests. These include compensation, personal financial benefits, investigations, discipline, or removal. Those matters follow the applicable disinterested decision procedures and the Chain of Authority.

The Articles of Incorporation’s Member-conflict restrictions apply automatically, without a vote to establish a conflict. The Member is excluded from the Eligible Seats for those matters. The Articles’ express exceptions for formation, succession, and Founder reclamation remain applicable.

For other conflicts involving the Founding Director or President, the Determination of Conflict rules apply, including the three-fourths threshold for determining whether a conflict exists.

Removal and Distinct Capacities

The Member serves without a fixed term or Board renewal, subject to the Articles of Incorporation. Voluntarily leaving one capacity does not itself end another. The Member resigns by written notice delivered to the President and Secretary, authenticated under the same standards that apply to a Director’s resignation.

Removal requires Just Cause, clear and convincing evidence, and unanimous approval by participating Disinterested Voting Directors. Eligible participation must meet the Quorum Governance threshold before Mission Fulfillment Conversion and, after it, every Mission Fulfillment Board position occupied and participating. The Leadership Accountability requirements for notice, evidence, hearing, and due process apply. Lawful disagreement or refusal to consent alone is not Just Cause.

If the Founder is also the Member, removing the Founder for Just Cause under this standard also removes the Member.

Member removal for Just Cause permanently ends that person’s Director positions, offices, ordinary return rights, and succession designations. A new Member is selected under the Successor Designation and Vacancy Selection provisions below. The removed person may return only through the last-resort recall in the Catastrophic Board Loss procedures after every other option, including court relief, is exhausted.

Successor Designation and Deadlines

Each Member shall designate one eligible Designated Successor and one eligible Designated Alternate within one year after taking office. Each designation and signed acceptance must be contained in a confidential Board Resolution. The incumbent at adoption has one year from the amendment’s effective date. No discretionary Board confirmation is required.

On each designation anniversary, the Member shall obtain and preserve each designee’s written confirmation of continuing willingness, qualification, identity, and reachability. A missing confirmation begins a thirty-day correction period rather than immediately ending the designation.

The Member may replace either designee through a new confidential Board Resolution and the replacement’s signed acceptance. The existing designation remains effective until the replacement record is complete and confirmed by both record custodians.

Death, disqualification, written refusal, failed contact after required efforts, or failure to cure the annual confirmation within thirty days makes a designation unavailable. If an emergency occurs during the correction period, service requires current verification and the applicable written acceptance.

Vacancy Selection

Upon death, adjudicated Permanent Incapacity, written resignation, or another succession condition established by these Bylaws, the eligible and willing Designated Successor succeeds first, followed by the Designated Alternate. Each person must accept the Member position in writing through the required restoration Board Resolution.

If neither designee can serve, the Chain of Authority selects an eligible candidate and records why each prior pathway failed. The first level able to act lawfully shall vet the candidate, obtain signed acceptance and conflict disclosures, and preserve its written appointment determination.

Selection under the Chain stops at the first level able to act lawfully. It confers only the Member’s reserved powers and no Founder, Founder Emeritus, Director, Corporate Officer, or Interim President status. The serving Member’s first restoration responsibility is establishing the President, Secretary, and Treasurer positions.

Acting Service and Return

Acting Member service begins upon the incumbent’s signed activation notice or objective inability certification under the Chain of Authority. The designated successor serves, or the alternate if necessary. Health-based certification requires independent medical evidence. No separate Board activation vote is required.

The Acting Member holds all reserved powers, subject to existing limits. It cannot change the incumbent’s membership, succession list, acting powers, termination of acting service, or return rights. It cannot judge or obstruct its own disputed authority.

Voluntary activation ends upon written return notice. Certified incapacity ends upon documented recovery certified under the Chain of Authority through the same objective process. Disagreement, refusal, absence, or delayed correspondence alone does not establish incapacity.

These Bylaws and nonwaivable law govern independent removal proceedings, temporary Acting Member service, return, and other termination events.

Founder Reclamation

A living Founder never removed for Just Cause may reclaim membership by signed notice under the Founder-return rules. The maximum age for Member service applies. Successor membership and acting authority then end.

This personal right is separate from return to a Pillar position under the Founder Emeritus rules. Both Founder return and Member reclamation remain subject to the applicable maximum age. Neither right passes to successors. Each successor must accept the membership-reclamation limitation in writing. Pending investigations and lawful restrictions survive reclamation.

Succession Records

The Secretary shall keep the sealed official copy of each designation and acceptance, and the Independent Review Panel shall keep a protected continuity copy. Access and every access attempt must be logged. If either custodian cannot act, the Chain of Authority governs lawful access and continuity.

The custodians shall preserve designations, acceptances, annual confirmations, replacements, access logs, activation notices, eligibility checks, deadlines, and final outcomes as confidential succession records. Activation permits only the disclosure necessary to establish the serving Member; all unrelated protected information remains confidential.

Director Inspection Rights

Directors may inspect and copy corporate books, records, financial reports, and governing documents to fulfill fiduciary duties and other office responsibilities. This includes investigating matters within the Board of Directors’s authority. Access must be reasonably necessary and follow reasonable Board procedures and applicable legal restrictions.

Disclosure is not required for:

  1. attorney-client privileged information or attorney work product
  2. legally protected personal information, confidential whistleblower reports, or cybersecurity safeguards
  3. information whose disclosure is unlawful or would materially harm the Nonprofit’s legitimate interests

Directors must protect confidential information received unless disclosure is required by law or these Bylaws.

Confidentiality safeguards shall not be used to conceal misconduct or obstruct proper Director oversight. Access shall preserve applicable legal protections.

Official Records

The Nonprofit shall create, maintain, preserve, and protect complete, accurate, secure corporate records under these Bylaws, the Articles of Incorporation, and applicable law.

The Board of Directors shall keep official records organized, secure, and reasonably accessible, supporting transparency, accountability, historical preservation, and institutional continuity. Records include governing documents, Board Resolutions, minutes, financial records, filings, policies, contracts, and other records required by law or these Bylaws.

Records shall use Board-approved formats, including secure electronic formats, and remain authentic, retrievable, protected against unauthorized alteration or destruction, and preservable for future generations.

These Bylaws, Technology and Records Standards, and applicable Board Resolutions govern records management, retention, technology platforms, archives, cybersecurity, version control, digital preservation, and public access.

Every report, disclosure, review summary, or other reporting component that these Bylaws require annually shall be presented together in one Annual Governance Report for the applicable fiscal year. Each component shall remain separately identifiable.

The Secretary shall coordinate assembly and publication after the required annual components are complete. Consolidation does not change any component’s required content, preparer, reviewer, approval, confidentiality, privilege, redaction rules, or underlying deadline. Quarterly, baseline, event-triggered, special, and completion reports are excluded unless these Bylaws independently require an annual summary.

Confirmed Receipt occurs at the earliest of the recipient’s acknowledgment, documented personal delivery, or reliable carrier or platform confirmation of delivery.

If none occurs, receipt is deemed twenty-four elapsed hours after successful documented transmission to the second of at least two current, previously verified communication channels, provided no failure or rejection notice has been received for either transmission. A failed, rejected, obsolete, or misdirected channel does not count.

A communication channel is verified when the recipient has confirmed it or successfully used it for official communication. It remains current until the recipient replaces it or failed delivery or reliable contrary information invalidates it. The Secretary or other assigned notice administrator shall preserve the verification evidence, delivery records, failure notices, and calculated receipt time.
