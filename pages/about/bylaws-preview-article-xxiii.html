Article XXIII is an Eternity Clause and protected by the Articles of Incorporation. It imagines a world where this work succeeds, and prepares for it long in advance. E Pluribus Unum answered the question of 1787: How do thirteen states become one nation? Ex Uno Floremus asks the question that comes afterward: Now that we are One, what shall we become? This Article turns that question into an obligation. When that day finally arrives, the Nonprofit lets go of Constitution v2.0 at the very moment holding on would be the easiest. What remains is a Museum, an Archive, a Watchdog, and the brightest light this world has ever known.

Section 23.1Ratification

E Pluribus Unum — “Out of Many, One” — answered the question our Founding Fathers faced in 1787: How could thirteen separate states become a single nation? ReDrafting America exists to ask the question that follows: Now that we are United, what is that one nation to become?

Ex Uno Floremus — “From One, We Flourish” — is ReDrafting America’s answer and its Official Motto. National Unity was never the destination of the American Experiment.

It was only the beginning of it.

This Article is where that motto stops being an aspiration and starts being an obligation.

These Bylaws already govern ReDrafting America’s beginning and its ending. This Article governs a third possibility neither addresses: that the Nonprofit accomplishes exactly what it was created to do. A Constitutional Convention convened under Article V of the Constitution of the United States will eventually be called, and Constitution v2.0 will be proposed there. If the states ratify it, ReDrafting America, and the country, will face the most consequential moments in their history. This Article exists so that moment is met with a solid plan the Board committed to in advance. It is not one the Board of Directors is asked to invent under pressure.

This plan is ReDrafting America’s answer, made concrete rather than merely aspirational. An organization whose entire mission is the peaceful adoption of a new constitution owes something to itself, its Directors, and the public it serves. It must know — in advance, and without ambiguity — what it will do if that mission succeeds. Every provision that follows describes, in exact and binding terms, how ReDrafting America pursues Ratification. It describes what it becomes once Ratification is achieved, and what the moment means once it has passed into history. Nothing here is left to be decided later, in the middle of the very transformation it describes.

As such, this Article is written for two audiences at once. To the Directors who govern this Nonprofit, it is a binding set of obligations. To the public who may one day encounter this document, it is a promise, made in advance: It says what kind of institution ReDrafting America intends to be today, what it will be in the future if its work succeeds, and what becomes of it afterward.

These triggers identify when the Nonprofit must prepare for, participate in, and respond to the national constitutional process. Each trigger activates the duties assigned to that stage.

A later trigger carries forward every uncompleted duty activated by an earlier trigger. The Board of Directors shall record each determination and preserve its supporting evidence in the Constitutional Records.

Section 23.1.1Imminence Trigger

An Active State has a pending legislative application asking Congress to call a convention under Article V of the United States Constitution. The legislature must not have rescinded, withdrawn, or otherwise revoked that application.

An Imminence Trigger occurs when thirty Active States exist or Congress formally calls an Article V convention. The qualifying applications must support a general convention or one compatible with Constitution v2.0.

The Board of Directors may recognize an earlier Imminence Trigger when credible evidence establishes that a compatible convention is imminent. Its Board Resolution must explain the expected convention, timing, evidence, and compatibility with Constitution v2.0.

Section 23.1.2Convention Trigger

A Convention Trigger occurs while a convention convened under Article V of the United States Constitution is in session. Constitution v2.0, or a substantially derived document, must be properly before the convention for consideration.

The Board of Directors shall determine substantial derivation through a Board Resolution. Its findings must identify material continuity with the proposal’s structure, protected rights, and constitutional institutions.

Section 23.1.3Adoption Trigger

An Adoption Trigger requires Constitution v2.0, or a substantially derived document, to become the legally effective United States constitution. The trigger occurs only when one of the completion conditions below is also satisfied.

Completion occurs at the earlier of these events. The first satisfied condition establishes the trigger date.

  1. twelve months after the formal ratification announcement, without a credible legal or governmental challenge remaining pending
  2. final resolution of every such challenge in favor of validity

After receiving qualified legal advice, the Board of Directors shall determine whether a completion condition has been satisfied. The Board Resolution must record the trigger date and preserve supporting evidence in the Constitutional Records.

Section 23.2Institutional Independence

Independence protects the Nonprofit’s charitable judgment, public credibility, and ability to complete its Mission without outside control. It applies throughout constitutional development, ratification, transition, and the Mission Fulfillment Phase.

These safeguards govern relationships with governments, Ratify America, funders, contractors, and former Official Representatives. They permit lawful cooperation while preserving separate authority, resources, records, and accountability.

Section 23.2.1Nongovernmental Status

The Nonprofit remains a private, independent, nongovernmental organization. No trigger, Bridge Provision, agreement, transfer, recognition, funding relationship, or cooperative activity changes that status.

No government body or official may appoint or remove the Nonprofit’s leaders. They may not exercise voting, veto, supervisory, or governance authority over the Nonprofit.

External regulation, lawful judicial authority, and ordinary contract enforcement do not constitute organizational control. The Nonprofit shall comply with each without surrendering its institutional independence.

Section 23.2.2Government Agreements and Funding

Every government agreement must preserve the Nonprofit’s separate legal identity, governance, finances, personnel, records, assets, and decision-making authority. It must not create an agency, partnership, joint venture, instrumentality, or employment relationship unless the Bylaws expressly authorize it.

Government grants, contracts, services, and cooperative activities require documented arm’s-length terms and fair value when applicable. No condition may control the Mission, constitutional work, governance, educational content, public findings, or criticism.

The Board of Directors shall disclose every material government funding relationship to the extent permitted by law. The disclosure must identify its purpose, value, material conditions, and independence safeguards.

Section 23.2.3Editorial and Historical Independence

No government body, official, contractor, funder, or other outside party may control the Nonprofit’s public work. Prohibited control includes approval, direction, editing, censorship, delayed publication, suppression, or compelled favorable treatment.

This protection covers The Museum, The Watchdog, the Constitutional Drafting Archive, civic education, historical accounts, and every publication governed by this Article. The Nonprofit retains final authority over methods, conclusions, wording, timing, exhibits, collections, and archival descriptions.

Outside parties may submit factual corrections, evidence, or public responses. The responsible program shall evaluate them under the same standards applied to comparable sources.

Material corrections must be preserved, and substantive revisions must be disclosed transparently. Access, cooperation, or funding does not create editorial authority, ownership, sponsorship, or endorsement.

Section 23.2.4Public Attribution and Non-endorsement

The Nonprofit shall not use governmental names, seals, credentials, facilities, or communications in a manner implying governmental status or unauthorized endorsement. Communications concerning cooperative work must identify the Nonprofit’s independent role.

The Nonprofit shall accurately distinguish its work from Ratify America’s work. Neither organization may be presented as the other’s agent, department, instrumentality, subsidiary, or controlled affiliate.

Every public product must identify its responsible author or publisher. Government access, assistance, or response does not make the government a sponsor or coauthor.

Section 23.2.5Post-Service Separation

A Pillar Director may begin formal or compensated government service only after completing every remaining Nonprofit obligation and resigning from the Board of Directors. Board service and government service may not overlap.

The government alone determines the former Director’s role, duties, supervision, duration, and compensation. The former Director serves individually and does not represent or bind the Nonprofit.

The Nonprofit shall not negotiate, direct, supervise, fund, or control the engagement. It shall not receive compensation or provide personnel, funds, accounts, credentials, or other resources for it.

A separate government contract with the Nonprofit must satisfy government agreement requirements. It cannot alter the former Director’s independent status or reopen the completed Pillar Director position.

A former Director serving the government may not participate in The Watchdog’s deliberations, methods, findings, or publications concerning that government. The person receives no nonpublic drafts, privileged materials, or preferential access.

Before completion is certified, qualified, conflict-free tax counsel shall review the separation plan. The review must address independence, conflicts, compensation separation, private benefit, inurement, and charitable-resource use.

Every interested Director must disclose the relationship and recuse from related deliberations and votes. Required independent review and reassignment shall follow the Chain of Authority.

Section 23.3Constitutional Readiness

Constitutional readiness requires a complete proposal and a lawful plan for moving from the existing Constitution to the new constitutional order. The Nonprofit shall prepare both before presenting Constitution v2.0 for national consideration.

The Bridge Mandate governs the transition plan, and certification confirms that the complete proposal is ready. A contingency process accelerates unfinished work without reducing any required safeguard.

Section 23.3.1The Bridge Mandate

Constitution v2.0 must define how the nation operates after adoption and how the lawful transition occurs. The requirements governing that transition are the Bridge Provisions.

The Bridge Provisions must provide a lawful, continuous, and publicly understandable transition. They must address, at minimum:

  1. convention delegate apportionment, selection, credentialing, voting, and quorum procedures
  2. the ratification method and its supporting theory of legitimacy
  3. the effective date, implementation stages, and standards for completing each stage
  4. a transitional executive office, including its term, authority, limits, succession, and removal
  5. a prohibition against extending or renewing the transitional office’s authority
  6. continuity of existing law, protected rights, courts, and legal remedies without creating a legal vacuum
  7. continuity or replacement of federal officeholders, including the judiciary
  8. legislative, administrative, and fiscal authority during the transition
  9. continuity of military, intelligence, and emergency services, with loyalty to the constitutional order
  10. transfer of public records, assets, obligations, and pending proceedings between outgoing and incoming institutions
  11. state-government harmonization with the new constitutional structure
  12. contested, disputed, or incomplete transitions, including standards for any good-faith transitional amnesty
  13. the amendment process governing Constitution v2.0 after adoption

Together, the Bridge Provisions must protect lawful authority, institutional continuity, public services, individual rights, and democratic legitimacy. They must also identify responsibility, sequence, deadlines, completion standards, and remedies for material deviation.

Section 23.3.2Convention-Ready Certification

Constitution v2.0 may receive Convention-Ready certification only through the governing release procedure. Certification confirms that the exact identified text satisfies every applicable readiness requirement.

The Board of Directors shall apply its approved checklist and complete every required review, audit, validation, and approval. It must determine that the text contains every required Bridge Provision.

The Steward must provide affirmative written approval before certification becomes effective. Required Independent AI Review analyses remain advisory and possess no certification authority, vote, veto, or governing power. The certification record shall identify the applicable review tier under the Artificial Intelligence Article and any subject-specific requirements.

The Board of Directors shall address every material concern, dissent, assumption, and unresolved issue in the certification record. Any artificial-intelligence override must follow the governing review procedure and appear in that record.

Required Constitutional Review Panel approval must be complete for amendments incorporated after the Panel activates. No certification may bypass an approval assigned to that body.

Certification attaches only to text identified by title, version, date, and a cryptographic hash or comparably reliable integrity identifier. The Nonprofit shall publish and preserve the checklist, analyses, evidence, issue dispositions, approvals, and Board Resolution with the certified text.

Any material revision ends the certification and requires a complete new determination, including Steward approval. A review of proposed but unapproved replacement text does not alter the certification of the unchanged exact version.

The Board of Directors shall review continuing certification at least annually. The review shall determine whether the exact certified version continues to satisfy every material readiness requirement and shall document its evidence, findings, dissent, and unresolved concerns.

If the continuing-certification review documents a material failure, the Board of Directors shall suspend certification by Board Resolution. The resolution shall identify the exact version, failure, effect, corrective steps, and a reasonable correction period when correction is possible. The Nonprofit shall promptly publish the resolution and update every public status label.

The Board of Directors may withdraw certification by Board Resolution when the material failure cannot be corrected without a material revision or remains uncured after the stated correction period. Restoration after suspension or withdrawal requires a complete new certification determination, including affirmative written Steward approval.

Section 23.3.3Readiness Contingency

When an Imminence Trigger occurs before certification, completing Constitution v2.0 becomes the Nonprofit’s highest institutional priority. Legal duties and essential operational continuity remain fully effective.

The same priority applies when credible evidence shows that an Article V convention will likely consider constitutional reform. The Board of Directors shall record that determination and its supporting evidence through a Board Resolution.

The Board of Directors shall adopt a written acceleration plan identifying unmet requirements, responsible reviewers, resources, dependencies, and target dates. It shall direct the Steward and required reviewers to expedite every remaining task.

Acceleration changes timing and resource priority only. It cannot waive, reduce, replace, or presume satisfaction of any required review, approval, validation, safeguard, or certification standard.

The Nonprofit shall not present, submit, or endorse Constitution v2.0 at a convention before certification becomes effective. This restriction applies even when certification cannot be completed before the convention concludes.

Section 23.4Flourishing

Ex Uno Floremus does not end at the moment of ratification — it begins there. A nation does not flourish by adopting a new constitution. It flourishes by living well under one. This Section describes what ReDrafting America becomes once Ratification is achieved — the point at which the promise embedded in this Article’s title comes due. The commitments that follow are not gestures of goodwill to be reconsidered once the mission has succeeded. They are obligations undertaken now, by the Directors who wrote them, on behalf of the Directors who will one day carry them out.

Section 23.4.1The Flourishing Fund

The Nonprofit’s post-adoption commitments require resources secured before those commitments become due. The restricted Flourishing Fund provides the financial foundation for the Mission Fulfillment Phase.

The Flourishing Fund supplements annual planning, fundraising, insurance, grants, and other prudent financing. Insufficient funding activates the shortfall process and does not independently cancel any commitment.

Section 23.4.2Fund Establishment and Stewardship

The Board of Directors shall establish and maintain the dedicated Flourishing Fund no later than the required formation date for Ratify America. The Fund finances post-adoption commitments and their reasonable direct administration.

The Fund shall not finance ordinary operations. Establishment and maintenance are standing obligations unaffected by any impracticability exception governing ordinary financial reserves.

The Treasurer shall maintain the Fund in a separate account or clearly segregated ledger under the Nonprofit’s financial controls. The Fund must remain separate from general operating reserves.

The Board of Directors may authorize prudent investments that preserve principal, purchasing power, liquidity, and timely availability. No Fund asset may be encumbered, pledged, borrowed against, or used as collateral.

The Board of Directors shall review the Fund annually using a written estimate of every commitment’s expected cost and timing. The estimate must address inflation, material risks, available balances, expected contributions, and reasonable contingencies.

The Board of Directors shall adjust the target balance when those factors materially change. If funding falls below target, it shall adopt a corrective plan with sources, responsibilities, and completion dates.

Section 23.4.3Fund Capitalization and Contributions

The Board of Directors shall capitalize the Fund through budget allocations, campaigns, grants, bequests, planned gifts, unrestricted gifts, investment earnings, and other lawful sources. Every source must comply with the Nonprofit’s Donation Ethics requirements.

An annual capitalization plan must connect the target balance with expected commitment dates. It shall identify intended sources, responsible Corporate Officers, expected amounts, deadlines, and material funding risks.

The plan must seek reasonable source diversity and avoid material dependence on one donor or funding source. The Board of Directors shall update it when costs, timing, risks, or expected contributions materially change.

No contribution may confer governance influence or control over constitutional work, The Museum, transition commitments, or recipient selection. The Board of Directors shall decline restrictions conflicting with the Mission, governing documents, post-adoption commitments, or law.

Assets restricted to the Fund remain dedicated to its purpose. They may be released, returned, redirected, or repurposed only when required by law or valid donor restrictions.

Section 23.4.4Expenditures and Financial Controls

Before an Adoption Trigger, disbursements are limited to reasonable costs of administering and protecting the Fund. Post-adoption spending must directly support an authorized commitment or its reasonable administration.

Each post-adoption disbursement requires a Board Resolution identifying the commitment, amount, recipient, purpose, and applicable financial controls. The resolution must also record material restrictions and conflicts.

Every disbursement remains subject to the Nonprofit’s budgeting, authorization, documentation, conflict-review, and accounting controls. Restricted assets may be spent only for their lawful designated purposes.

The Treasurer shall preserve a complete Fund ledger and supporting records. Financial reporting must distinguish contributions, earnings, expenses, commitments, restrictions, available balances, and approved shortfall measures.

Section 23.4.5Resource Shortfalls and Scope Reduction

A Resource Shortfall exists when available Fund assets and committed lawful funding cannot fully support a post-adoption commitment when due. The Treasurer shall document its amount, cause, timing, effects, alternatives, and material assumptions.

The Board of Directors may declare a Resource Shortfall through a Board Resolution after reviewing that analysis. It shall publish the declaration and findings, subject to lawful confidentiality protections.

The Board of Directors shall address a Resource Shortfall in the following order. Each step must preserve the affected commitment as fully as available resources permit:

  1. extend performance timelines while preserving the commitment’s full scope
  2. conduct time-limited supplemental fundraising while sequencing continues
  3. reduce scope temporarily only as a last resort without eliminating the commitment

Before reducing scope, the Board of Directors shall obtain independent financial and legal review through the Chain of Authority. Approval requires three-fourths of Voting Directors, the required Quorum, and written Steward approval.

Published findings must explain why sequencing and supplemental fundraising are insufficient. They must establish the reduction’s duration, review schedule, restoration conditions, and responsible Corporate Officers.

The Board of Directors shall review every reduction at least quarterly and restore full performance as soon as reasonably achievable. A reduction expires when its stated conditions end unless renewed through the same process.

Unauthorized diversion or avoidable neglect does not excuse corrective accountability. No shortfall response may evade law, donor restrictions, creditor rights, fiduciary duties, or this Article’s amendment requirements.

Section 23.5Introducing Ratify America

The Nonprofit shall prepare for ratification without exceeding its charitable authority or controlling another organization’s advocacy. Ratify America is the intended independent organization for lawful public, legislative, and political advocacy supporting constitutional reform and ratification.

Formation must establish complete institutional separation before advocacy begins. Later dealings must preserve that separation through independent governance, arm’s-length terms, accurate public representations, and distinct operations.

Section 23.5.1Mission and Founding Goals

Ratify America’s organizing documents shall establish the following Mission Statement. It defines the organization’s focused founding mission:

The primary mission of Ratify America shall be the peaceful advocacy, civic mobilization, legislative engagement, and lawful ratification of Constitution v2.0 as a modern United States Constitution. It shall be founded upon the principles of truth, liberty, accountability, democratic governance, and the long-term flourishing of the American people.

The organizing documents shall also establish the following founding goals. They describe the intended program without limiting consistent lawful methods:

  1. build nationwide public support through accurate and accessible advocacy
  2. organize peaceful civic participation
  3. advocate before federal, state, convention, and ratification decision-makers
  4. build a broad coalition supporting consideration, adoption, and ratification
  5. promote a lawful, transparent, and democratic ratification process
  6. conduct a large-scale national advertising campaign across every lawful and reasonably available form of media

Section 23.5.2Formation and Initial Governance

Upon an Imminence Trigger, the Board of Directors shall complete every action within its control to form Ratify America as an independent nonprofit corporation. It shall act promptly and complete its formation duties within thirty days. The intended organization shall seek recognition under Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(4).

External processing delays do not violate that deadline when every required submission was timely. The Board of Directors may begin earlier through a Board Resolution finding that formation is reasonably warranted.

The organizing documents shall make promoting social welfare the organization’s primary purpose. They shall incorporate the approved Mission Statement, founding goals, independence safeguards, and applicable tax and election-law requirements.

The initial governing body shall consist of seven outside directors. The Nonprofit’s eligible Disinterested Directors shall appoint them after review by the Independent Review Panel.

The initial governing body must collectively possess substantial knowledge or experience in the following areas. The required competencies are:

  1. constitutional and Article V law
  2. nonprofit and Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(4) governance
  3. election and campaign-finance law
  4. communications and advertising
  5. coalition building and civic organizing
  6. finance and operations
  7. ethics and public accountability

No individual must satisfy every competency. The appointment record must identify how the full governing body satisfies all seven areas.

The initial governing body and organizing documents shall control every later appointment, succession, activity, continuity measure, and dissolution. The Nonprofit receives no post-formation appointment, removal, voting, veto, or supervisory power.

Section 23.5.3Institutional Separation

Ratify America is the Nonprofit’s sister organization and shares its commitment to constitutional reform. Each organization shall remain legally and operationally independent. The Nonprofit shall neither control Ratify America nor submit to its control.

No Official Representative may hold a corresponding Ratify America position at formation. Except as expressly authorized for formation, written transactions, asset transfers, and intellectual-property licenses, the organizations shall not share personnel or operational resources. The organizations must maintain separate legal identities, governance, finances, accounts, records, personnel, operations, communications, property, credentials, and systems.

The Nonprofit shall not direct, supervise, coordinate, subsidize, or control Ratify America’s advocacy, finances, personnel, activities, or decisions. Formation does not expand the Nonprofit’s authority to lobby, conduct political activity, or finance advocacy.

No funds, services, or resources may be routed through Ratify America to perform activities prohibited to the Nonprofit. No Nonprofit resource may support candidate intervention or the separate political entity.

Every transaction, license, shared service, or transfer between the organizations requires a lawful written agreement approved after conflict review. The agreement must use fair value when applicable and document responsibilities, costs, records, and control.

The Nonprofit may pay reasonable formation expenses only through a Board Resolution and written approval from qualified tax counsel. The approval must establish charitable purpose, reasonable cost, proper records, and no continuing control.

Neither organization may represent the other as its agent, subsidiary, department, instrumentality, or controlled affiliate, or act for, bind, or authorize the other. Agreements and joint communications must identify their separate roles and disclaim unauthorized agency or control.

Reviewers designated under the Chain of Authority shall review compliance with these separation requirements at least annually and report their findings to the Board of Directors.

Section 23.5.4Constitutional Scope and Recommendations

Ratify America may advocate for Constitution v2.0, its official revisions, and substantially derived constitutional text approved by the Constitutional Review Panel. It may not independently designate revised or derived text as authorized campaign text.

Ratify America may analyze constitutional developments and recommend changes. Its only path for those recommendations is submission to the Nonprofit’s Constitutional Review Panel.

The Constitutional Review Panel shall evaluate each recommendation through the Nonprofit’s constitutional process. Ratify America may advocate for changed text only after the Panel grants approval.

Ratify America’s directors, officers, key employees, paid campaign leaders, and affiliated political-entity officials may not serve as voting members of the Constitutional Review Panel. They may provide transparent, nonvoting testimony or liaison assistance.

Ordinary support, volunteer service, or donations do not alone disqualify a Panel member. The member must disclose relevant relationships and recuse when required by conflict procedures.

Every publication must distinguish official text, approved derived text, pending recommendations, and independent commentary. Nothing grants Ratify America ownership or official revision authority over Constitution v2.0.

Section 23.5.5Advocacy and Campaign Boundaries

The organizing documents shall authorize Ratify America to conduct forceful, peaceful advocacy within its focused mission. Its independent governing body shall control its methods, positions, communications, expenditures, and legal compliance.

Authorized activities may include lobbying, grassroots mobilization, coalition building, paid advertising, petitions, convention advocacy, and ratification advocacy. The organization may use every lawful and reasonably available communications medium.

Candidate endorsements and other campaign intervention must remain secondary activities. They may not become Ratify America’s primary activity while it operates under Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(4).

Before candidate-election activity becomes recurring or primary, the organizing documents shall require a legally separate Internal Revenue Code § 527 political organization or political action committee. That entity must maintain the governance, finances, records, reporting, and compliance required by applicable law.

The Nonprofit shall not approve, direct, coordinate, finance, subsidize, or veto Ratify America’s advocacy or candidate decisions. It shall not control or support the separate political entity.

Every public communication must identify the responsible organization accurately. Shared history, compatible purposes, or lawful transactions do not create agency, control, or authority to speak for another organization.

Section 23.5.6Independent Funding and Donor Notice

The Nonprofit may provide only the approved formation expenses and transferred assets authorized by this Section. After formation, Ratify America shall raise, control, account for, and spend its own funds.

Ratify America shall establish independent fundraising, banking, accounting, budgeting, and financial controls before substantive advocacy begins. Its funds may not be deposited in, routed through, guaranteed by, or administered through Nonprofit accounts.

Every fundraising solicitation and contribution receipt must prominently state that contributions generally are not deductible as charitable contributions. Additional tax, election, lobbying, and donor disclosures must follow applicable law.

The separate Internal Revenue Code § 527 political organization or political action committee must maintain its own legally required financial controls. Neither the Nonprofit nor charitable resources may fund its candidate-election activities.

Section 23.5.7Intellectual Property and Brand License

The Nonprofit retains ownership, authorship records, and official revision authority for Constitution v2.0 and its related Foundational Works. Formation of Ratify America transfers none of those rights.

The Nonprofit shall grant Ratify America a written, nonexclusive, royalty-free, mission-limited license. The license may cover official constitutional text, approved marks, campaign materials, and other property reasonably necessary for the focused mission.

The license shall establish permitted uses, attribution, version control, objective brand standards, records, and end-of-mission procedures. Brand protections may preserve source identification and quality but may not create control over Ratify America’s advocacy decisions.

Ratify America owns original campaign materials it independently creates, excluding content and brand elements licensed from the Nonprofit. Those materials must distinguish its advocacy from the Nonprofit’s official constitutional work.

The license shall narrow or end when the authorized constitutional objective ends, as its written terms provide. Reasonable archival and historical uses may continue when they preserve attribution and do not imply current authorization.

Section 23.5.8Ratify America Asset Transfer

Before formation, the Nonprofit owns the ratifyamerica.org domain and operates the website at

https://www.ratifyamerica.org

It may maintain other assets reserved specifically for Ratify America.

Those assets remain segregated from the Nonprofit’s charitable programs before transfer. They may not support advocacy while the Nonprofit controls them.

Within ninety days after formation, the Nonprofit shall transfer the domain, website, Ratify America branding, and every other Ratify America-specific asset. The transfer must include related property, accounts, records, credentials, licenses, and operational materials.

The Board of Directors shall approve a written transfer agreement and complete asset inventory. The transfer record must document delivery, independent acceptance, continuing obligations, restrictions, and any consideration exchanged.

Qualified, conflict-free counsel shall confirm the transfer’s lawful tax treatment and any fair-value requirements. Interested Directors shall disclose their interests and recuse from deliberations and decisions.

A refusal, inability, or failure to accept the assets activates the rejected-handoff process. Until final disposition, the Nonprofit shall preserve them securely without conducting Ratify America’s intended advocacy.

Section 23.5.9Mission Viability and Disposition

Until ratification, Ratify America’s governing body shall conduct and document an annual mission-viability review. It shall evaluate material progress, barriers, resources, legal developments, public support, strategy, and the continued feasibility of ratification.

Formal rejection, an expired ratification effort, or prolonged inactivity does not automatically end the mission. The governing body may continue, pause, change lawful strategies within scope, or begin an orderly wind-down.

Each decision requires written findings and a public summary subject to lawful confidentiality protections. A paused campaign remains subject to financial, records, compliance, and annual-review requirements.

Ratification completes Ratify America’s original founding mission. Thereafter, its independent governing body controls its lawful wind-down, continuation, or redirection under its governing documents.

Any continuation or redirection must comply with applicable law and may not create authority over the Nonprofit or Constitution v2.0. Existing liabilities, restrictions, records duties, and contractual obligations survive mission completion.

Section 23.5.10Convention Participation

When a Convention Trigger occurs, Ratify America is the intended independent organization for lawful convention advocacy. The Nonprofit remains limited to education, scholarship, research, and public information consistent with its Charitable Purposes.

Before participating, the Board of Directors shall adopt a written convention compliance plan reviewed by qualified, conflict-free attorneys. The plan must define permitted education, prohibited advocacy, approvals, financial controls, records, and public disclosures.

The Board of Directors shall update the plan whenever convention procedures or applicable law materially change. Material activity outside the approved plan requires advance legal review.

The Nonprofit may publish educational materials, answer public and press inquiries, and provide factual resources to delegates and the public. Its materials must remain accurate, educational, nonpartisan, and clearly attributed.

The Nonprofit shall preserve its convention materials, approvals, legal advice, staff time, and related expenditures. These records must demonstrate compliance with its charitable authority and institutional separation.

The Nonprofit shall not spend or route funds to influence delegate selection, delegate elections, or convention votes. It shall not direct, finance, coordinate, supervise, subsidize, or control Ratify America’s advocacy.

Once Constitution v2.0, or approved substantially derived text, is properly before the convention, the Nonprofit retains no special authority over it. No Official Representative possesses an organizational veto or continuing control over the text under consideration.

An Official Representative may participate independently but must state that the participation is personal. That person may not imply Nonprofit endorsement or use Nonprofit resources for personal convention activity.

An individual serving in a formal or compensated convention role must disclose that role to the Board of Directors. The Nonprofit shall record any resulting Conflict of Interest and apply its conflict procedures.

Disclosure gives the Nonprofit no authority over the individual’s convention activity. If a Convention Trigger precedes Ratify America’s operations, every Nonprofit limitation remains effective. The Nonprofit cannot assume Ratify America’s intended advocacy role while formation continues.

Section 23.6Mission Fulfillment Governance

An Adoption Trigger begins the Mission Fulfillment Phase and changes the Nonprofit’s governing priorities. Temporary transition duties must be completed while permanent work is organized around The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog.

The Flourishing Fund supports these programs and their reasonable administration. A controlling Mission Fulfillment Plan coordinates the transition from the existing Board of Directors to the permanent Mission Fulfillment Board. After conversion, that Board of Directors operates under Legacy Governance.

Section 23.6.1Mission Fulfillment Phase

During the Mission Fulfillment Phase, the Nonprofit shall complete every temporary duty created by constitutional adoption. It shall also establish the governance, resources, personnel, records, and safeguards required for continuing work.

The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog operate as distinct programs. The Watchdog remains editorially independent while evaluating constitutional implementation.

Temporary duties include the following work. Every duty remains active until completed, transferred, or resolved through an authorized contingency:

  1. implementing the Bridge Mandate and completing permitted government handoffs
  2. forming Ratify America and transferring its designated assets
  3. transferring, licensing, preserving, or retiring the Digital Constitutional Convention platform
  4. establishing The Archive and permanent public access
  5. establishing The Museum and its collections
  6. establishing The Watchdog’s independent operating capacity
  7. capitalizing the Flourishing Fund or approving a sustainable funding plan
  8. transferring every continuing responsibility held by a departing Pillar Director

The Nonprofit shall preserve clear responsibility for each unfinished duty throughout the transition. Governance conversion does not cancel, diminish, or conceal any remaining obligation.

Section 23.6.2Mission Fulfillment Plan

The Mission Fulfillment Plan converts this Article’s obligations into assigned work, deadlines, dependencies, budgets, evidence, and completion standards. It coordinates performance without creating new authority or weakening any requirement.

The Nonprofit shall begin preparing the Plan upon an Imminence Trigger and may begin earlier when justified. Before adoption, the Plan remains a readiness document reviewed regularly by the Board of Directors.

An Adoption Trigger activates the Plan. The Board of Directors shall then confirm its assignments, schedule, resources, dependencies, safeguards, and public-reporting process.

The Board owns and approves the Plan, and the President coordinates its execution during transition. Pillar Directors remain responsible for their assigned deliverables.

The Treasurer coordinates finances and the Flourishing Fund. The Secretary coordinates records, certifications, and preparations for The Archive.

The Director of Research and Education prepares The Museum, and the Director of Ethics and Oversight prepares The Watchdog for independent operations. Independent Directors monitor conflicts, independence, and completion certifications.

The Chain of Authority remains available throughout the Mission Fulfillment Phase. It first assigns unresolved matters to eligible Disinterested Directors who are Pillar Directors.

Before conversion, the matter then proceeds to an eligible Disinterested Director who is an Independent Director, but only when one-person action is authorized. If that level cannot act, the matter proceeds to the disinterested Independent Review Panel and finally to available court relief.

Escalation is permitted only when an earlier level lacks authority, is conflicted, lacks required participation, no longer exists, or cannot lawfully act. Disagreement or a failed vote alone does not permit escalation.

An express Mission Fulfillment assignment controls. Each escalation must record the matter, unavailable level, legal basis, acting level, decision, and supporting record.

The sequence cannot create authority, waive requirements, override Steward approval, or weaken Eternity Clause protections. The Mission Fulfillment Board assumes responsibility for the Plan after governance conversion.

The Plan must contain an obligations register, deliverables, completion standards, dependencies, responsibilities, succession assignments, schedules, handoff requirements, and rejection contingencies. It must also address finances, governance conversion, independence, records, information safeguards, risks, public reporting, and final disposition.

The Plan is an official organizational record. A public version shall report obligations, responsible offices, milestones, status, delays, expenditures, and completion determinations.

Protected schedules may contain personal information, privileged advice, security controls, credentials, protected negotiations, or information whose early release could disrupt a lawful transition. The public version shall identify each protected schedule’s existence and general purpose.

The Steward must approve the initial Plan and every material revision in writing. This approval does not restore authority over Constitution v2.0 after it enters the national constitutional process.

A material revision also requires recorded Board approval, an explanation of consequences, an updated public version, and preservation of the previous version. Administrative updates may be recorded without reopening the complete Plan.

An obligation is complete only when its required evidence exists and every necessary recipient accepts the transfer. When acceptance remains outside the Nonprofit’s control, completion requires every authorized action and activation of the applicable contingency.

After all temporary duties are complete, the Board of Directors shall publish a final Mission Fulfillment Report. Continuing responsibilities then enter ordinary Mission Fulfillment Board operations.

Section 23.6.3Transitional Board of Directors

The existing Board of Directors continues governing during the initial Mission Fulfillment Phase. It retains fiduciary authority and responsibility for completing or securely assigning every temporary duty.

Certified Pillar seat closures reduce the Board of Directors’s authorized size and require immediate recalculation of every quorum, participation requirement, and voting threshold. Those planned closures do not activate Minimum Governance restrictions.

An ordinary vacancy, removal, incapacity, or unexplained departure remains governed by the applicable lifecycle and Board Continuity Emergency requirements. Labeling a departure as mission completion cannot avoid those protections.

Before another certified closure would reduce the transitional Board below seven Directors, governance conversion must be completed. Conversion may occur earlier after every required condition is satisfied.

Section 23.6.4Pillar Completion and Seat Closure

A Pillar Director may complete a Pillar only after fulfilling every assigned Nonprofit obligation. Completion also requires an orderly transfer of all records, property, credentials, authority, reports, and unfinished work.

Every continuing duty must be assigned to a qualified person, program, or governing body. The disinterested Board of Directors shall certify completion in writing without the affected Director’s participation.

The Director’s resignation must become effective before any government service begins. Certified resignation closes every Pillar seat that does not continue on the permanent Mission Fulfillment Board.

A closed seat is not a vacancy, and no successor may be selected for it. A departure without completion certification creates an ordinary vacancy and leaves the Pillar seat open.

The President, Secretary, Treasurer, Director of Research and Education, and Director of Ethics and Oversight positions remain authorized after conversion. Completion of their earlier Pillar work does not close those continuing positions.

A departure from a continuing position creates an ordinary vacancy. The remaining open seats determine authorized Board size before conversion, and the five permanent positions determine it afterward.

Section 23.6.5Government Advisory Service

After an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit may notify the new government that a Pillar Director may become available for transition-advisory service. Any service must concern the Bridge Provisions and remain within that Director’s Pillar of Leadership.

The government alone decides whether to offer a role and determines its duties, supervision, duration, and compensation. No Director is promised, selected, or entitled to government service.

A Director may accept such service only after completing the applicable Pillar, receiving certification, and resigning from the Board of Directors. Government service and Board service may not overlap.

The former Director serves individually and may not represent or bind the Nonprofit. Compensation must be paid directly by the government and creates no revenue, asset, or financial interest for the Nonprofit.

The Nonprofit shall not select government advisors, negotiate or guarantee their compensation, subsidize an engagement, or receive a financial benefit from it. It shall not direct, supervise, fund, or control the government service.

Government service does not excuse any unfinished institutional commitment. The separation, conflict, recusal, and Watchdog safeguards governing post-service roles remain fully effective.

Section 23.6.6Mission Fulfillment Board

The Nonprofit may convert to the permanent Mission Fulfillment Board after the principal drafting mission is complete. Every temporary duty must also be completed or securely assigned.

Conversion also requires independent operations for The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog. An approved Flourishing Fund sustainability plan and lawful transition to the complete permanent Board of Directors are also required.

Conversion and the final noncontinuing Pillar seat closures take effect simultaneously. The permanent Mission Fulfillment Board has five authorized positions.

They are:

  1. the President
  2. the Secretary
  3. the Treasurer
  4. the Director of Research and Education
  5. the Director of Ethics and Oversight

All five positions remain Corporate Officers and Pillar Directors. The Secretary leads The Archive, the Director of Research and Education leads The Museum, and the Director of Ethics and Oversight leads The Watchdog.

The Mission Fulfillment Plan shall distribute every continuing responsibility from a departing Director or closed seat among the five continuing Board positions. The President oversees organization-wide coordination, administration, and the relationship with external legal counsel. The Secretary oversees The Archive, records, and certifications.

The Treasurer oversees finances and the Flourishing Fund. The Director of Research and Education oversees The Museum, Ratification Day, and civic education. The Director of Ethics and Oversight oversees The Watchdog and internal ethics. The Independent Review Panel provides independent review of conflicts, independence, and completion.

The Board of Directors may assign performance to qualified personnel, programs, or authorized bodies, but one continuing Director must remain accountable for each responsibility. No duty lapses because its former seat closes.

Every other Pillar seat closes only after its assigned obligations are completed or securely transferred. This includes the Director of Legal Affairs and Compliance position.

Before conversion, all three Independent Directors may continue serving through their protected terms, and conversion may not remove an Independent Director before term expiration. Conversion may occur only after every Independent Director seat has closed through term expiration or voluntary resignation. No Independent Director position continues after conversion.

Upon conversion, Legacy Governance begins, the Board of Directors’s authorized size is five, and ordinary Quorum is three. Below three qualified, onboarded, occupied positions, authority is limited to preservation, emergency response, legal compliance, existing obligations, and restoration. Compensation changes, bylaw amendments, major transactions, and voluntary dissolution are prohibited.

The Mission Fulfillment Board exercises the Nonprofit’s fiduciary and corporate authority. It establishes policy, approves budgets, protects independence, appoints Corporate Officers, and holds organizational leadership accountable.

Professional personnel operate The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog under their applicable independence safeguards. The Board of Directors may oversee administration and performance without directing protected editorial conclusions.

After conversion, every matter these Bylaws assign to Independent Directors proceeds to the disinterested Independent Review Panel for final conflict-free determination. That includes officer compensation, related-party transactions, audit oversight, complaints concerning senior leadership, and participation in leadership removal proceedings. For audit oversight, the Panel shall engage a qualified independent accountant who reports directly to it.

Before conversion, the Board of Directors shall adopt a fixed Independent Review Panel fee schedule supported by an independent compensation consultant’s written benchmark. After conversion, the schedule changes only to match a new written benchmark from an independent compensation consultant. No Director may approve or adjust it.

These assignments preserve the prohibition on reciprocal compensation approval. Neither body may approve its own compensation.

The Nonprofit retains its existing external legal counsel for ordinary legal services. Counsel receives no Board seat or governance authority through that engagement.

Conflicted or specially assigned governance matters remain subject to the Independent Review Panel and Chain of Authority. The Mission Fulfillment Board shall preserve both safeguards.

Upon conversion, the Constitutional Review Panel consists of every Mission Fulfillment Board member and every regular Independent Review Panel member. The earlier internal and outside seat structure then ends.

The participants act as one Other Body and cast one vote each in their Constitutional Review Panel capacity. No duplicate Board of Directors or Independent Review Panel vote follows.

Every authorized Constitutional Review Panel position must remain occupied and eligible from proposal through effectiveness. Amendment approval requires unanimous participating Constitutional Review Panel approval and separate affirmative written Steward approval.

After ratification, the Constitutional Review Panel may amend only the Nonprofit’s organizational Eternity Clauses. Amendments to the national constitution follow the ratified constitution’s procedures.

Section 23.7Our Enduring Legacy

After an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit’s continuing work rests on three permanent programs: The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog.

Their relationship mirrors that of the National Archives and the Smithsonian Institution. The National Archives keeps the nation’s founding documents as evidence. The Smithsonian helps the public understand why they matter. In the same way, The Archive preserves the past as it happened, The Museum explains the past as it matters, and The Watchdog examines the present as it is kept.

Each program remains distinct in purpose, leadership, and professional standards, and each strengthens the others. The Museum draws every exhibit from records The Archive preserves. The Archive allows anyone to verify what The Museum presents. Both supply The Watchdog with the evidence and public understanding its work requires.

The Chief Governance Officer leads The Archive, the Chief Scholarship Officer leads The Museum, and the Chief Integrity Officer leads The Watchdog. These programs continue for as long as the Nonprofit can responsibly sustain them.

Section 23.7.1The Archive

The Archive is the Nonprofit’s permanent memory. It preserves the complete record of the Nonprofit and Constitution v2.0 exactly as it happened, so that anyone may verify what was done, said, and decided. The Chief Governance Officer leads The Archive.

Section 23.7.1.1Archive Mission and Holdings

The Archive holds, at minimum:

  1. the Constitutional Drafting Archive, including drafting records, Drafting Room materials, artificial-intelligence verification records, the Digital Constitutional Convention record, and ratification and Ratification Day records
  2. the Nonprofit’s permanent governance records, including the Articles of Incorporation, every official version of these Bylaws, minutes, resolutions, voting records, and meeting recordings
  3. The Watchdog’s reports, methods, government responses, independent reviews, and corrections
  4. other records designated for permanent preservation

Records in any format belong to The Archive. Physical objects belong to The Museum. When an item is both, such as a signed original document, The Archive holds it and may lend it to The Museum under written terms protecting preservation and chain of custody.

The Archive keeps every record within its scope. It does not select records by significance, reputation, or preferred narrative.

Section 23.7.1.2Record Integrity

No preserved record may be altered. Corrections, context, and disputes are added as documented annotations beside the original.

The Archive shall maintain a written records-management policy governing record scope, acquisition, ownership, provenance, authenticity, custody, and preservation. The policy must also govern security, conservation, digitization, restrictions, loans, reproduction, correction, and deaccession.

Core constitutional and institutional records may not be sold for revenue. They may not be removed because they are embarrassing, critical, unpopular, disputed, or inconsistent with a preferred narrative.

Deaccession is permitted only for defined collection-management reasons. Those reasons are limited to the following circumstances:

  1. lawful return to a person or institution entitled to the material
  2. material outside the approved collection scope
  3. redundant copies whose removal does not impair the historical record
  4. irreparable deterioration or a documented safety or security necessity
  5. transfer to a more qualified repository providing equal or stronger protection

Every material deaccession requires recorded justification, conflict review, and a permanent disposition record. Any proceeds must support acquisition, preservation, conservation, or direct collection care.

Digital records must retain reliable provenance, integrity information, version history, and chain of custody. Preservation copies shall use durable formats and migrate when necessary without obscuring originals or later alterations.

Confidential, privileged, private, and security-sensitive materials remain protected under law and the Bylaws. Restrictions must be documented, reviewed, and removed when their lawful basis ends.

Section 23.7.1.3Access Duty and Covered Records

Upon an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit assumes a continuing duty to provide meaningful public access to records entrusted to it. The duty protects historical understanding, constitutional accountability, civic learning, and informed public participation.

Access must remain durable, affordable, searchable, and usable across physical and digital settings. Lawful restrictions must remain narrow while preservation and succession controls protect long-term availability.

The access duty continues while the Nonprofit possesses the records or controls the responsible program. Transfer, reorganization, technological change, or reduced resources does not extinguish that duty.

Permanent public access covers the releasable portions of the following records. The list establishes the minimum scope without excluding other records designated for public use:

  1. the Constitutional Drafting Archive
  2. certified Constitution v2.0 texts and their supporting public records
  3. the public Digital Constitutional Convention record and Ratification Day materials
  4. civic education resources and related public educational materials
  5. The Watchdog’s reports, methods, government responses, independent reviews, and corrections
  6. public Mission Fulfillment Plan, performance, expenditure, and completion records

Public access does not confer authority to alter a record or control conclusions issued by The Museum or The Watchdog. Users may analyze, criticize, and interpret accessible materials independently.

Section 23.7.1.4Lawful Access Restrictions

Access may be restricted only to protect personal information, privilege, security-sensitive information, intellectual-property rights, valid donor restrictions, preservation needs, or another legal obligation. Every restriction must be no broader or longer than necessary.

The Nonprofit shall prefer redaction, summaries, delayed release, or supervised access when those measures provide adequate protection. Complete withholding is permitted only when narrower measures cannot protect the governing interest.

Every material restriction must be recorded, justified, and reviewed periodically. The Nonprofit shall explain the restriction publicly to the fullest lawful extent.

No donor, government body, former Director, contractor, or other outside party may purchase or condition suppression of an otherwise public record. Funding, cooperation, access, or services cannot justify alteration, delayed release, or an improper paywall.

Section 23.7.1.5Preservation and Recovery

The Nonprofit shall maintain redundant preservation copies, secure backups, current inventories, format-migration procedures, and documented chains of custody. Preservation must not depend on one building, website, vendor, device, or administration.

Recovery plans must identify responsible personnel, systems, credentials, priorities, and restoration procedures. The Nonprofit shall test those plans periodically and correct material weaknesses.

Domains, accounts, repositories, encryption keys, and administrative credentials require documented organizational control and succession. No individual may retain exclusive control over a required access channel or preservation copy.

A temporary outage does not end the access duty. Restoration or an equivalent access method must receive priority based on public need, record significance, security, and available resources.

Section 23.7.1.6Digital Access and Affordability

The Nonprofit shall maintain a stable official digital repository with a searchable catalog. Core public records must be readable online and downloadable in durable, commonly usable formats when lawful and technically practicable.

Each published record must retain sufficient provenance, date, version, and correction information. These details must distinguish the authoritative record from later copies, revisions, or interpretations.

Core digital access shall remain free to the public. The Nonprofit may charge reasonable fees for specialized assistance, high-cost reproduction, licensing beyond ordinary public use, or optional programs.

Fees may not prevent reasonable access to the core constitutional and institutional record. Appropriate waivers must be available when cost would materially limit meaningful public access.

Section 23.7.1.7Physical and Accessible Use

The Nonprofit shall provide reasonable physical access to original materials when preservation, security, privacy, and lawful restrictions permit. Access procedures must protect both users and collections.

Public materials must be usable by people with disabilities and compatible with commonly used assistive technologies. The Nonprofit shall address known material accessibility barriers within reasonable operational and preservation constraints.

When physical access is unavailable, the Nonprofit shall provide a substantially equivalent digital or remote method whenever practicable. An alternative must preserve meaningful use rather than merely acknowledge the record’s existence.

Section 23.7.1.8Access Continuity and Accountability

If the Nonprofit cannot maintain an access channel, it shall establish an equivalent channel or arrange qualified external custody. A current preservation copy must remain protected throughout the transition.

An external custodian must be a qualified public, academic, archival, or nonprofit repository. Selection and approval shall follow the Chain of Authority and require written acceptance of the continuing access duty.

Any transfer, reorganization, or dissolution affecting covered records must preserve that duty through an enforceable written agreement. Refusal or failed acceptance activates the rejected-handoff process.

The Nonprofit shall publish an annual public-access report. It shall address additions, digitization, availability, restrictions, restoration, requests, accessibility improvements, preservation risks, transfers, and material interruptions.

The report must identify corrective work, responsible offices, target dates, and unresolved limitations. Lawfully protected information may be summarized without exposing the protected material.

Section 23.7.1.9Custody of Originals

After an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit shall offer the complete Constitutional Drafting Archive to the National Archives and Records Administration or Library of Congress. It may offer custody to both institutions.

The Archive includes artificial-intelligence verification records, Drafting Room materials, and every other record documenting the development of Constitution v2.0. Drafting Room materials are records created through the collaborative constitutional-drafting process and its working environment. External custody of original records does not end The Archive. The Archive continues to maintain preservation copies, finding aids, and public access for as long as the Nonprofit exists, and The Museum continues to interpret the record.

The Board of Directors shall prepare an inventory and transfer plan protecting authenticity, provenance, chain of custody, digital integrity, preservation formats, and accessibility. The transfer must preserve origins, authorship, deliberations, revisions, and public participation.

Privileged records, personal information, security-sensitive materials, and lawfully restricted records must be identified and protected before transfer. Public access shall remain as broad as those protections, intellectual-property rights, donor restrictions, and law permit.

Transfer is complete only after the recipient accepts custody in writing and the Board of Directors verifies successful delivery. The Nonprofit shall retain a secure preservation copy until verification occurs.

If neither federal institution accepts custody, the Board of Directors may select a qualified public or academic repository through the Chain of Authority. The repository must provide substantially equivalent preservation, integrity, security, and public-access protections.

External custody grants no current or former Official Representative authority to interpret or control Constitution v2.0. The transfer preserves history and creates no continuing claim upon the constitutional text.

Section 23.7.2The Museum

After an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit shall establish its headquarters as a public museum and civic education institution. The Museum turns the record The Archive preserves into public understanding. It is the permanent home of the Nonprofit’s historical exhibits, educational programs, and civic learning. The Chief Scholarship Officer leads The Museum.

The Museum operates as a public trust rather than a memorial to any person, administration, institution, or preferred historical account. Its collections and programs must preserve context, encourage learning, and support informed citizenship.

Section 23.7.2.1Museum Mission and Collections

The Museum shall explain the complete history of the Nonprofit, Constitution v2.0, ratification, and the constitutional transition. It shall document how the work developed and what followed from it.

Its collections shall include the following materials. The list describes the minimum scope and does not exclude other historically significant records or objects:

  1. artifacts, significant objects, oral histories, and interpretive digital materials
  2. artificial-intelligence exhibits and representative development materials
  3. Ratification Day exhibits, civic education resources, and educational publications
  4. exhibits explaining public participation, professional review, institutional decisions, implementation, and continuing public consequences

The Museum shall explain the new constitution’s institutions, rights, responsibilities, and civic expectations. Its programs must remain accurate, nonpartisan, accessible, and open to good-faith critical examination.

Every exhibit shall draw on records preserved by The Archive and identify its sources so that visitors may verify them.

Section 23.7.2.2Curatorial Governance

The Board of Directors governs The Museum, approves its budget and policies, appoints qualified leadership, and holds that leadership accountable. A professionally qualified Curator directs collections, preservation, interpretation, and exhibits.

During the initial Mission Fulfillment Phase, the Chief Scholarship Officer shall serve as Curator. The Curator shall protect collection integrity, provenance, historical context, accurate attribution, and distinctions between primary records and later interpretation.

The Curator shall coordinate with the Chief Governance Officer on records drawn from The Archive. Public engagement, digital access, and facilities remain with their assigned Pillar Directors until those duties transfer.

The Chief Scholarship Officer’s transitional Museum assignment cannot be certified complete until The Museum is operational. Its collections, records, systems, credentials, and continuing responsibilities must also be transferred securely.

The disinterested Board of Directors shall certify that completion without the Chief Scholarship Officer’s participation. Afterward, the Board of Directors may appoint a qualified professional Curator who is not a Director.

A later Curator appointment transfers operating responsibility without closing the Chief Scholarship Officer’s permanent Board position. It creates no authority to alter the certified governance transition.

Section 23.7.2.3Collection Stewardship

The Museum shall maintain a written collections-management policy governing collection scope, acquisition, ownership, provenance, authenticity, custody, and preservation. The policy must also govern security, conservation, digitization, restrictions, loans, reproduction, correction, and deaccession.

Significant objects may not be sold for revenue. They may not be removed because they are embarrassing, critical, unpopular, disputed, or inconsistent with a preferred narrative.

Deaccession is permitted only for defined collection-management reasons. Those reasons are limited to the following circumstances:

  1. lawful return to a person or institution entitled to the material
  2. material outside the approved collection scope
  3. redundant copies whose removal does not impair the historical record
  4. irreparable deterioration or a documented safety or security necessity
  5. transfer to a more qualified repository providing equal or stronger protection

Every material deaccession requires recorded justification, conflict review, and a permanent disposition record. Any proceeds must support acquisition, preservation, conservation, or direct collection care.

Confidential, privileged, private, and security-sensitive materials remain protected under law and the Bylaws. Restrictions must be documented, reviewed, and removed when their lawful basis ends.

Section 23.7.2.4Historical Integrity and Attribution

The Museum shall preserve achievements, disagreements, criticism, rejected proposals, mistakes, corrections, limitations, and unresolved questions that materially illuminate the historical record. It shall not present commemoration as complete historical analysis.

Interpretation must distinguish primary sources from later commentary and verified facts from disputed accounts. Material uncertainty, missing evidence, and competing supported interpretations must be disclosed.

Authorship and contributions must be attributed accurately. Collective work may not be assigned solely to the Founder, a Director, donor, institution, or artificial-intelligence system.

The Board of Directors may require accuracy, sound methods, lawful conduct, financial controls, and compliance with Museum policies. It may not knowingly falsify, conceal, destroy, suppress, or materially distort the historical record.

No donor, government body, former Official Representative, or outside party may purchase favorable treatment or require omission of material history. Access, funding, prestige, or cooperation creates no right to control interpretation.

The Museum shall correct material factual errors openly while preserving earlier versions and the correction history in The Archive. It shall evaluate relevant evidence submitted by the public, researchers, participants, and institutions.

An annual stewardship report shall describe acquisitions, preservation, access, loans, deaccessions, corrections, restrictions, programs, expenditures, and significant risks. The report must disclose material conflicts while protecting lawfully restricted information.

Section 23.7.2.5Artificial Intelligence Exhibit

The Museum shall maintain a permanent exhibit explaining how artificial intelligence contributed to Constitution v2.0 and the Bylaws. The exhibit shall use verification records preserved by The Archive and representative prompts, outputs, revisions, reviews, and decisions.

For each participating system, the exhibit shall identify its provider, model, role, and period of use when available. It shall explain material limitations, errors, disagreements, concerns, safeguards, and lessons learned.

The exhibit must distinguish artificial-intelligence assistance from human authorship, judgment, approval, and accountability. It shall neither assign collective work solely to a system nor operate as a commercial endorsement.

The Board of Directors may withhold privileged records, personal information, security-sensitive materials, and lawfully restricted records. The exhibit shall identify the general reason for each material omission when lawful.

Exhibit content must remain accurate and accessible. It shall be updated when preserved records or verified historical understanding materially change.

Section 23.7.2.6Exhibit Access

The Museum shall provide reasonable public access to its exhibits and programs, both in person and online. Online exhibits and core educational programs shall remain free to the public. The Museum may charge reasonable fees for admission, specialized programs, or high-cost services, but appropriate waivers must be available when cost would materially limit meaningful access. Exhibits and programs must be usable by people with disabilities and compatible with commonly used assistive technologies.

Section 23.7.2.7Ratification Day

Each year after an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit shall observe the ratification anniversary as Ratification Day. The Museum shall host the observance with reasonable physical and digital public access.

Programming shall commemorate ratification and examine the constitution’s history and operation. It shall teach civic rights and responsibilities while encouraging informed participation.

Every observance must remain accurate, nonpartisan, accessible, and open to good-faith critical examination. Commemoration may not suppress supported disagreement, limitations, or later historical understanding.

If The Museum is unavailable, the Nonprofit shall provide an equivalent program elsewhere or online. Educational materials and recordings from each observance shall enter The Archive.

Ratification Day shall continue while the Nonprofit exists. Resource constraints may change its format without eliminating its educational and commemorative purposes.

Section 23.7.3The Watchdog

The Watchdog provides independent, evidence-based public accountability after constitutional adoption. It evaluates whether governmental implementation remains faithful to Constitution v2.0 without becoming a political advocate or government instrument.

Its work begins with intensive transition monitoring and continues through annual constitutional accountability reporting. Every report becomes part of The Archive’s permanent record.

The Chief Integrity Officer leads The Watchdog.

Section 23.7.3.1Watchdog Mission and Independence

The Watchdog shall evaluate constitutional implementation, institutional performance, protected rights, public responsibilities, and material departures from the constitutional design. It shall distinguish constitutional accountability from ordinary political disagreement.

The Watchdog possesses editorial and analytical independence within the Nonprofit. Its methods, findings, conclusions, publication timing, and corrections cannot be controlled for political, financial, governmental, or reputational convenience.

The Mission Fulfillment Board may approve budgets, appoint leadership, establish lawful policies, require sound methods, and address misconduct or nonperformance. It may not dictate conclusions, require favorable treatment, suppress supported criticism, or condition funding on outcomes.

Neither The Museum, The Archive, donors, government officials, former Directors, nor the Steward may direct or approve Watchdog findings. The Archive may preserve, and The Museum may interpret, published records without controlling the underlying accountability work.

Before governance conversion, the Chief Integrity Officer leads Watchdog preparation and reporting. Afterward, the same officer oversees the program subject to its editorial independence.

A vacancy shall not excuse or delay required reporting. The Chief Executive Officer shall appoint a qualified interim preparer, and every conflicted preparer requires recusal and conflict-free replacement.

Section 23.7.3.2Implementation Watch Period

The Implementation Watch Period begins upon an Adoption Trigger and provides enhanced monitoring throughout the constitutional transition. It continues until the required completion conditions are satisfied.

Those conditions require completion of every Bridge stage and operation of the principal constitutional institutions. Federal and state responsibilities must also be substantially harmonized.

Unresolved transitional authority must be transferred or terminated. Initial constitutional elections and succession processes must be complete, followed by one full ordinary governmental operating cycle.

The Watchdog shall recommend when these conditions have been met. The Mission Fulfillment Board makes the final determination through recorded findings supported by the public implementation record.

Ending the Implementation Watch Period ends enhanced quarterly reporting only. It does not end The Watchdog, annual reporting, public accountability, record preservation, or correction duties.

Section 23.7.3.3Material Implementation Concerns

A Material Implementation Concern requires credible evidence of a significant constitutional implementation failure, departure, impairment, or materially inaccurate public representation. It may concern acts, omissions, delays, substitutions, or unsupported claims.

A concern exists when a required institution or safeguard is absent or materially incomplete. It also exists when a constitutional deadline or Bridge milestone is materially missed.

Other concerns include authority lacking its required constitutional foundation and transitional authority exceeding its purpose. Material impairment of a protected right, remedy, election, succession, or accountability mechanism also qualifies.

Loss, destruction, concealment, or wrongful withholding of essential records constitutes a concern. A demonstrably inaccurate material statement about implementation also qualifies.

The Watchdog shall document the evidence, affected requirement, public consequences, responsible authority, and available corrective action. It may issue a special report without awaiting the next scheduled publication.

Section 23.7.3.4Reports and Dashboard

At the beginning of the Implementation Watch Period, The Watchdog shall publish a baseline report. The report shall identify the principal institutions, Bridge Provisions, responsible bodies, expected sequence, completion standards, starting conditions, and unavailable information.

During that period, The Watchdog shall publish quarterly implementation reports and maintain a current public implementation dashboard. It shall also publish an annual comprehensive Constitutional Accountability Report and any necessary special reports.

The dashboard must show each monitored obligation, responsible body, expected milestone, present status, supporting evidence, material delay, and unresolved concern. It must distinguish verified completion from reported, disputed, partial, or unverified completion.

After the Implementation Watch Period, quarterly reports may end, but the annual report continues while the Nonprofit exists. The public dashboard shall remain available as a permanent historical record.

Review shall cover constitutional institutions, Bridge Provisions, rights, remedies, services, records, elections, appointments, succession, state harmonization, and transfers of authority. It shall also evaluate constitutional limits, measurable public outcomes, substitutions, incomplete requirements, and corrective commitments.

Section 23.7.3.5Evidence, Responses, and Corrections

Every report shall identify its evidence, methodology, material limitations, and unresolved factual disputes. It must distinguish verified facts, analysis, opinion, and recommendations while presenting serious competing interpretations fairly.

The report must disclose relevant conflicts and recusals. It shall not endorse candidates, political parties, or electoral outcomes.

Before publication, each report shall receive the Independent AI Review tier classified under the Artificial Intelligence Article and any necessary legal or subject-matter review. The preparer shall record the tier, reasons, every identified material concern — a governance, ethical, legal, constitutional, structural, or operational issue that could reasonably affect the Nonprofit or Constitution v2.0 — and its disposition.

Every unresolved material concern shall be published with the report. Any Director may require the Board of Directors to review its classification before publication.

The affected government shall receive a reasonable opportunity to submit a written response by a stated deadline. A timely response shall accompany the report, subject only to lawful redactions.

Government silence does not delay publication. Any unresolved disagreement between the Board of Directors and the preparer shall be disclosed with the report.

Citizens, officials, researchers, journalists, organizations, and affected communities may submit evidence or concerns. The Watchdog independently determines what warrants investigation, and submissions confer no authority over findings.

The Watchdog shall correct material errors openly and preserve every published version. Corrections must identify the error, supporting evidence, affected conclusions, correction date, and responsible reviewer.

The Nonprofit shall publish reports, methods, responses, reviews, and material corrections in accessible formats. It shall preserve every edition and its supporting record in The Archive.

Section 23.7.3.6Independent Watchdog Review

The Watchdog shall receive periodic independent review of its methodology, accuracy, impartiality, corrections, conflicts, accessibility, and compliance with published standards. The reviewer shall be appointed through the Chain of Authority.

Reviewers must possess relevant expertise and remain independent of the reviewed work, affected governments, donors, and interested Official Representatives. Every material conflict requires disclosure, recusal, and replacement.

The review evaluates the integrity of the process without approving or rewriting individual conclusions. It shall identify strengths, material weaknesses, corrective recommendations, and any unresolved disagreement with The Watchdog.

The Mission Fulfillment Board shall publish the review and a written response. It shall assign corrective actions, deadlines, and responsible officers without compromising protected editorial independence.

Section 23.8Handoffs and Substitutions

The Nonprofit shall complete each required handoff through documented transfer, acceptance, and verification. Every handoff must preserve the affected property, records, purpose, safeguards, and public obligations throughout the transition.

Recipient refusal or legal impossibility does not authorize abandonment or a false completion claim. Interim custody, renewed offers, and equivalent substitutes protect the obligation until lawful disposition becomes possible.

Section 23.8.1Handoff Standards

Before a material handoff, the Board of Directors shall approve a written transfer plan and complete inventory. The plan must identify the property, records, rights, restrictions, responsibilities, costs, liabilities, safeguards, schedule, and intended recipient.

The Board of Directors shall obtain qualified technical, financial, privacy, security, accessibility, records, intellectual-property, and legal assessments appropriate to the transfer. Every reviewer must disclose material conflicts and remain independent of interested parties.

A written agreement must establish delivery, custody, ownership, licenses, continuing obligations, acceptance standards, and remedies. It must preserve institutional independence and prohibit unauthorized agency or continuing control.

personal information may transfer only when the transfer is lawful, necessary, secure, and consistent with its collection purpose. Required notice, consent, minimization, retention, access, and deletion protections remain effective.

A handoff is complete only after the recipient accepts responsibility in writing and the Board of Directors verifies successful delivery. The Nonprofit shall preserve the agreement, evidence, approvals, conflicts, and verification in its permanent records.

Every material handoff requires written Steward approval before completion. That approval confirms compliance with this Article without granting control over the recipient’s later lawful conduct.

Section 23.8.2Convention Platform Handoff

After an Adoption Trigger, the Nonprofit shall offer the Digital Constitutional Convention platform to the new government. The intended use is official civic-participation infrastructure under independent governmental control.

The Board of Directors may authorize a transfer, license, or other lawful arrangement. Approval requires qualified technical, privacy, security, accessibility, records, intellectual-property, and conflict-free legal review.

The agreement must identify transferred technology, intellectual property, records, data, licenses, support, costs, liabilities, and operational responsibilities. It must also establish transition dates, continuity protections, security requirements, accessibility standards, and written acceptance.

The Nonprofit shall preserve an authoritative copy of the platform’s historical public record in The Archive. The handoff creates no continuing organizational authority over governmental use, policy, administration, or later platform development.

If the government rejects the platform, the Nonprofit shall preserve its technology and authoritative public record. It shall then apply the rejected-handoff and equivalent-substitution requirements.

Section 23.8.3Rejected Handoff Contingencies

A rejected handoff occurs when an intended recipient refuses, cannot accept, or fails to accept a complete offer after reasonable follow-up. Rejection also occurs when acceptance requires unlawful terms or weakened safeguards.

Rejection does not complete the obligation, transfer ownership, authorize abandonment, or permit control of the intended recipient. The Nonprofit shall retain secure interim custody and preserve all affected property and records.

Continued operations must remain limited to protection, continuity, lawful public access, and preparation for final disposition. They cannot be used to assume the intended recipient’s independent mission or authority.

The Board of Directors shall determine whether a remediable defect caused the rejection. The Nonprofit shall correct defects within its control and renew the offer when lawful and consistent with the original purpose.

The Nonprofit need not accept conditions weakening required protections or institutional independence. If renewed acceptance remains unavailable, the Board of Directors shall begin the equivalent-substitution process.

For each rejection, the Board of Directors shall preserve the offer, response, follow-up, assessments, conflicts, alternatives, approvals, and final disposition. It shall publish a summary subject to lawful privacy, privilege, confidentiality, and security protections.

Completion may be certified only after every authorized action is performed and the approved fallback is activated. A delayed recipient decision does not prevent certification after those conditions are satisfied.

Section 23.8.4Equivalent Substitutions

An equivalent substitute may be considered when the original handoff or performance method cannot lawfully or practicably succeed. Qualifying barriers include refusal, impossibility, illegality, safety, privacy, privilege, cybersecurity, restricted gifts, creditor rights, and insolvency.

The Board of Directors shall use the Chain of Authority to identify the authorized, conflict-free decision-maker. The acting body must satisfy its governing participation and approval requirements.

A substitute must advance the original obligation’s purpose and preserve every essential safeguard to the greatest practicable extent. Written Steward approval is required before the substitution becomes effective.

For rejected Ratify America assets, the substitute must preserve separation and avoid advocacy by the Nonprofit. The assets may transfer to a qualified independent successor or be preserved or retired securely.

For a rejected Convention platform, the Nonprofit may offer qualified public, academic, or nonprofit custody. It may instead provide a lawful public license, limited nonofficial access, or secure preservation and retirement.

For the Archive, The Archive retains secure custody and provide the greatest sustainable lawful access. The Nonprofit shall continue seeking qualified external custody when doing so remains useful.

Every substitution requires documented efforts, barriers, alternatives, conflicts, reviews, approvals, expected effects, and continuing obligations. No substitution may evade law, fiduciary duties, restrictions, independence, preservation, public access, or this Article’s amendment requirements.

Section 23.9Continuing Public Purpose

The Nonprofit shall continue only while it provides a distinct public benefit that it can perform responsibly. Periodic independent review protects that standard against institutional habit, self-preservation, financial pressure, and changing public needs.

The review may recommend continuation, correction, restructuring, protected transfer, merger, or dissolution. It cannot itself authorize an action assigned to another governing process.

Section 23.9.1Review Cycle and Scope

The Mission Fulfillment Board shall conduct a Continuing Public Purpose Review at least once every five years after governance conversion. It may begin earlier when a material change warrants review.

Relevant changes include program performance, capacity, finances, legal status, institutional independence, public need, or the availability of a better-qualified steward. An early review must identify the material change and its expected consequences.

The review shall determine whether the Nonprofit continues providing a distinct public benefit through The Archive, The Museum, The Watchdog, and civic education. It shall evaluate both individual programs and their combined institutional value.

The review must examine Mission relevance, performance, public need, governance capacity, financial sustainability, charitable compliance, duplication, independence, preservation risks, and access. It must also consider whether another qualified organization could protect a continuing program more effectively.

Section 23.9.2Independent Evaluation

The Board of Directors shall initiate the review, but an independent evaluator shall conduct or supervise the analysis. The evaluator must be selected through the Chain of Authority.

The evaluator may retain qualified museum, archival, constitutional, nonprofit, financial, technical, or public-interest expertise. Every participant must disclose material conflicts and recuse when independence is impaired.

The evaluator shall review relevant organizational, programmatic, financial, legal, performance, preservation, and public-access records. The Nonprofit shall provide reasonable access subject to lawful protections.

Staff, former Directors, researchers, public institutions, affected communities, and the public may submit relevant evidence. Participation informs the review but confers no governance or decision-making authority.

Section 23.9.3Findings and Corrective Action

The review shall consider continuation, corrective action, restructuring, affiliation, program transfer, merger, and dissolution. It shall evaluate each viable option against the Mission and continuing public obligations.

The review shall not presume that the Nonprofit must continue to preserve positions, employment, reputation, property, or accumulated assets. It shall not recommend closure because accurate history or supported accountability reporting is unpopular, critical, or inconvenient.

The evaluator shall publish its evidence, analysis, limitations, material disagreements, and recommendations, subject to lawful protections. The Board of Directors shall issue a recorded response and final determination.

Written Steward approval is required for the determination and every resulting material revision to the Mission Fulfillment Plan. Approval must precede implementation of the determination.

A decision to continue shall identify corrective work, responsible parties, resources, deadlines, and measures of success. The Board of Directors shall monitor and report progress until every accepted correction is complete.

Each review shall confirm that the Program Succession Plan is current.

If the Board rejects a material recommendation, it shall publish its reasons. It must conduct a focused independent follow-up review within one year.

Section 23.9.4Program Succession Plan

The Archive, The Museum, and The Watchdog remain with the Nonprofit for as long as it can responsibly sustain them.

The Board of Directors shall maintain a Program Succession Plan naming, for each program, at least one qualified successor custodian and one alternate. Each designation must be supported by the successor’s written expression of willingness, which is not binding. The Plan shall be reviewed during every Continuing Public Purpose Review and updated promptly when a designated successor becomes unavailable.

The Plan activates upon an authorized merger or dissolution. It may also activate for a single program when a Continuing Public Purpose Review finds that the program cannot be sustained responsibly and that correction and restructuring have failed.

A proposed program transfer must preserve the program’s purpose, records, independence, public access, restricted assets, and continuing obligations. The recipient must possess suitable governance, capacity, expertise, and financial sustainability.

The Watchdog may not transfer to a government body or another person it evaluates. Its recipient must preserve editorial independence, evidence standards, correction duties, public reporting, and historical records.

The Archive’s records and The Museum’s collections may transfer only to a qualified steward capable of preserving authenticity, integrity, provenance, context, security, and access. Lawful restrictions and collection-care obligations must remain effective.

Every transfer requires a written agreement, inventory, independent review, conflict disclosure, governing approval, and recipient acceptance. Failed acceptance activates the rejected-handoff and equivalent-substitution requirements.

No transfer may provide improper private benefit or evade donor restrictions, creditor rights, fiduciary duties, or charitable obligations. Restricted property must remain dedicated to its lawful purpose.

Section 23.9.5Merger and Dissolution Routing

The Continuing Public Purpose Review recommends and plans but does not authorize a merger, substantial asset disposition, or dissolution. Each action must satisfy its governing approval and review requirements.

Every resulting action remains subject to applicable law, donor restrictions, contracts, fiduciary duties, creditor rights, handoff safeguards, and continuing obligations. Required independent review and Steward approval remain fully effective.

The Board of Directors shall not preserve the Nonprofit merely for its own continued existence. When distinct public purpose cannot be sustained, it shall preserve public benefit through an authorized transfer, merger, or dissolution.

Any final action must protect The Archive’s records, The Museum’s collections, The Watchdog’s independence, restricted assets, and permanent public access. Completion requires documented disposition and written acceptance of every transferred continuing duty.
