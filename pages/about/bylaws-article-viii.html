Article VIII

Committees

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article VIII is about sharing the work without surrendering the responsibility. Some committees decide. Others study, advise, and recommend, and decide nothing at all. Every one must say in writing which it is, what it exists for, who sits on it, and what it may do. Some questions sit too close to the people deciding them, so they go outside entirely, to a panel of independent attorneys with no stake in the answer. One thing is refused outright: no small group may quietly become the real government of the Nonprofit between meetings. Good institutions hollow out that way. A board that delegates its judgment has not delegated. It has resigned.

Committee Purpose

The Board of Directors may establish committees to assist in carrying out the charitable mission of ReDrafting America. They promote effective governance, informed decision-making, specialized expertise, and efficient administration.

Committees exist to study issues, conduct research, oversee designated organizational functions, and develop recommendations. They perform investigations authorized by the Board of Directors and assist in implementing the lawful policies and strategic objectives of the Nonprofit.

Committees serve solely as instruments of the Board of Directors and shall operate in furtherance of the Nonprofit’s Charitable Purposes. The establishment of a committee shall not diminish the authority, fiduciary responsibility, or accountability of the Board of Directors. The Board of Directors retains ultimate responsibility for the governance and affairs of the Nonprofit.

Committee Formation

Unless a special committee provision controls, the Board of Directors may establish a committee by majority vote of Voting Directors with the required Quorum. The establishing resolution shall classify it as a Committee of the Board of Directors or an Advisory or Operational Committee. It shall specify:

  1. Purpose and membership
  2. Authority and limitations
  3. Reporting obligations and duration
  4. Meeting, records, confidentiality, and conflict requirements

A committee without a stated duration continues until dissolved. The Board of Directors may dissolve it at any time by majority vote of Voting Directors with the required Quorum, subject to special committee protections.

Unless the establishing resolution names a chair, the Board of Directors shall appoint one by majority vote of Voting Directors. The Board of Directors may remove and replace the chair, with or without cause, by the same vote. Removing the chair designation does not itself remove committee membership.

The Board of Directors appoints committee members unless its establishing resolution delegates appointment to an officer or body. Unless the resolution states otherwise, membership lasts for the committee’s duration. The Board of Directors may remove a committee member, with or without cause, by a two-thirds vote of Voting Directors with the required Quorum. Special committee protections remain controlling. Removal does not affect any independently held Director, officer, employee, or other position.

The Member cannot appoint, remove, or approve committee members or direct committee composition. The Board of Directors cannot delegate those powers to the Member.

Unless its establishing resolution provides otherwise, a committee follows the applicable Board meeting procedures, adapted to its size and function. These include notice, Quorum, minutes, recess, and adjournment.

Board Committees

A Committee of the Board of Directors may exercise only specified authority lawfully delegated to it. Only serving Directors may vote or exercise delegated Board authority. Others may assist solely as nonvoting advisers.

The establishing resolution may designate Directors or Corporate Officers as ex officio members, serving because they hold another office. Such members may vote only if they are serving Directors and the resolution does not make their membership nonvoting.

Unless a greater requirement applies, Quorum requires a majority of eligible committee members. Action requires a majority of eligible members present and voting. Prepare contemporaneous minutes, deliver them to the Secretary, and report them promptly to the Board of Directors. The establishing resolution cannot waive these minutes requirements.

A Committee of the Board of Directors shall not:

  1. Create or fill a Board vacancy
  2. Appoint, elect, confirm, suspend, or remove a Director, except to make nonbinding recommendations
  3. Adopt, amend, or repeal these Bylaws, or alter a resolution reserved to the Board of Directors
  4. Approve dissolution or another fundamental transaction, or alter the Eleven Pillars
  5. Exercise authority assigned to another body or prohibited from delegation by law or these Bylaws

No committee, including an Executive Committee, may hold standing authority to exercise the Board of Directors’s full powers between meetings. Urgent or exceptional action outside a duly noticed Board meeting must follow the Emergency procedures. Committee delegation cannot bypass those procedures.

A Committee of the Board of Directors may consider compensation, conflicts, discipline, removal, related-party transactions, or other matters requiring Disinterested Directors. Only eligible Disinterested Directors may deliberate or vote on those matters. The Board of Directors retains ultimate fiduciary responsibility and may modify or revoke delegated authority, subject to law and these Bylaws.

Authority conferred directly by these Bylaws is not a revocable Board delegation. Its specific Quorum, voting, recusal, recordkeeping, and oversight rules control. This includes the Compensation and Related-Party Committee’s final authority and the special committee’s limited authority under the Required Matters and Deadlock procedures.

The separate Independent Director stipend approval rules remain controlling. No committee may exercise final authority reserved to another body unless these Bylaws expressly authorize it.

Advisory and Operational Committees

Advisory and Operational Committees may include Directors, officers, employees, volunteers, advisers, contractors, experts, community representatives, and other qualified people. They may research, investigate, administer programs, coordinate activities, consult stakeholders, prepare reports and proposals, and perform authorized tasks outside governance.

These committees cannot exercise or claim Board authority. They cannot establish Board Quorum, vote as the Board of Directors, or appoint, suspend, or remove Directors. Powers reserved to Directors, governing bodies, or Corporate Officers remain outside their authority. An authorized officer or Board body must separately approve and execute any action binding the Nonprofit. Recommendations remain advisory until approved by an authorized body or officer.

Each committee shall maintain appropriate records and comply with confidentiality, conflicts, ethics, records-retention, privacy, and information-security requirements. Non-Director compensation and reimbursement follow the compensation rules and applicable Board-adopted policies.

Independent Review Panel

The Independent Review Panel provides outside review and decisions for matters expressly assigned by these Bylaws. It serves at its designated level of the Chain of Authority, subject to applicable authority, eligibility, conflict, participation, and approval requirements.

When conflicts prevent an authorized internal body from acting, an assigned matter may pass to eligible, Disinterested Independent Review Panel members. That transfer occurs only through the Chain of Authority. A disagreement, deadlock, or failed vote does not itself transfer authority.

The Independent Review Panel consolidates the outside governance review panels, including their assigned compensation responsibilities. The Constitutional Review Panel remains separate and exercises authority over Constitution v2.0 and Eternity Clause amendments under its own procedures.

Authority and Assignments

The Independent Review Panel is an “other body” under Pennsylvania nonprofit law, exercising only the authority expressly assigned by these Bylaws. It is separate from Committees of the Board of Directors and advisory committees. Binding determinations require an express grant of decision-making authority; advisory assignments confer no final approval power. A disagreement or failed vote does not itself transfer authority to the Independent Review Panel.

The Panel cannot supply missing Board votes or Quorum, or override powers reserved to another body. Its expressly assigned catastrophic Board-reconstitution authority remains subject to that procedure’s conditions and limits. No participant may review their own conduct, certify their own compliance, select their own reviewer, or approve their own compensation. Medical findings remain with qualified physicians, and legal services remain with appropriately licensed, qualified attorneys.

Any Official Representative may complain that a Member action violated the non-retaliation prohibition. The decision-makers designated under the Chain of Authority shall determine and certify whether a violation occurred. A certification of violation makes the action void from the outset. Any Director purportedly removed is automatically restored without further action.

The decision-makers designated under the Chain of Authority shall also certify when an investigation or removal proceeding concerning the Member has properly commenced. Certification suspends the Member’s Director appointment and removal powers for the duration of that matter. They shall record the certification date in the official corporate records.

Membership and Qualifications

The Independent Review Panel comprises five outside, independent attorneys and two qualified attorney alternates. The five regular seats have the following respective specialties, requiring demonstrated relevant experience. Overlapping expertise is permitted; no specialty confers an extra vote or exclusive authority.

  1. Constitutional law
  2. Nonprofit corporate governance
  3. Tax-exempt organizations and compensation
  4. Employment law and internal investigations
  5. Civil litigation and dispute resolution

Members and alternates must maintain appropriate attorney licensure and professional standing. They may not hold an internal role or have served as a Director, officer, employee, Member, or Acting Member within the preceding five years.

A spouse, domestic partner, parent, child, or sibling may not currently serve as a Director, officer, Member, or Acting Member. Relationships that materially compromise independent judgment disqualify a candidate or member. Relevant financial, employment, client, and business relationships must be disclosed before appointment, annually, and promptly upon change.

Conflicts must be assessed for each matter, with recusal whenever independent participation is impaired. Approved Independent Review Panel service, required Constitutional Review Panel service, and reasonable approved compensation for those roles do not alone disqualify a member. These arrangements do not permit self-review or approval of personal compensation.

Appointments and Terms

Recruitment shall begin at Quorum Governance. All five regular members and both alternates shall be appointed no later than the transition to Pillar Governance. These appointments may occur simultaneously with the final Pillar Director appointment.

Each appointment requires approval by three-fourths of all Disinterested Voting Directors, with the required Board Quorum. The Member and Acting Member shall not participate in the appointment decision.

Regular members serve three-year staggered terms. Initial terms are one year for two seats, two years for two seats, and three years for one seat. Subsequent terms last three years.

Alternates serve three-year staggered terms. Their initial terms are one year and two years, followed by three-year terms. These expiration dates establish staggering, not activation priority.

Terms may be renewed without a fixed limit. Every renewal requires a fresh qualifications and independence review and the same approval required for an initial appointment.

Meetings and Decisions

The Independent Review Panel shall follow Board meeting procedures, including recording, notice, agendas, deliberation, voting, minutes, publication, and records retention. Executive Session safeguards protect confidential proceedings and recordings. Express Independent Review Panel procedures and deadlines control where they differ from general Board procedures.

Substantive decisions require at least four eligible, disinterested participants and three affirmative votes. Vacancies, recusals, and abstentions do not reduce these requirements. Catastrophic Board-reconstitution appointments and emergency protective orders against protected Independent Directors require unanimity of eligible participants, with at least four participating.

The Panel shall elect a Chair from its regular members for a one-year renewable term. Election or replacement requires four eligible participants and three affirmative votes. Replacing the Chair designation does not remove Panel membership.

The Chair coordinates submissions, scheduling, deadlines, and records. The Chair cannot decide matters alone, block eligible submissions from reaching the Panel, cast extra votes, or override decisions. Routine scheduling does not require a substantive vote.

Authorized submitters shall have a direct, confidential submission channel without requiring permission from anyone under review. Record receipt, assigned authority, participants, conflicts, and deadlines. Give affected persons notice and a reasonable opportunity to respond before adverse findings, subject to authorized temporary protective measures.

Record the evidence considered, findings, reasons, votes, recusals, and required notices. Certification and notification shall occur within seven days after the triggering condition becomes objectively verifiable, unless an expressly different deadline applies. Protect confidential, privileged, and medical information; provide required notices to authorized recipients and preserve restricted corporate records.

Compensation and Legal Services

Eligible Independent Directors approve Pillar Director compensation under the compensation procedures. The Independent Review Panel replaces the outside reviewers for Independent Director stipends. Eligible Disinterested Pillar Directors approve Independent Review Panel compensation by three-fourths of all Disinterested Voting Directors, with the required Board Quorum.

Independent Review Panel agreements may provide reasonable availability retainers and approved fees for additional work. Agreements shall include required Constitutional Review Panel service, covered services, availability commitments, work rates, expenses, and spending limits. Regular members and alternates may receive different compensation according to their obligations.

Compensation and changes require advance approval, relevant market comparisons, and contemporaneous documentation of the basis for approval. The Member, Acting Member, recipients, and anyone with a personal or financial conflict shall not participate in approving Independent Review Panel compensation. Outcome-dependent fees, duplicate payment, and compensation changes intended to influence a pending matter are prohibited.

No participant may approve compensation for someone who approves that participant’s compensation. Independent Review Panel fallback authority applies only when its participants are independently eligible for the particular decision. The Panel’s fallback determination of Director compensation is not barred because Directors approve the Panel’s retainer. The deciding Panel members must have no other conflict in the matter. If neither authorized body can act, new compensation or changes must await eligible decision-makers or available lawful relief.

When legal services are needed, identify the responsible appropriately licensed, qualified attorney and document the client, scope, and conflict review. Eligible Panel attorneys may provide those services; outside specialists may assist when necessary. Approved agreements and spending limits apply.

During formation, the Founder acting as Member may arrange necessary corporate legal services and reasonable written fees. Subsequent disinterested review is permitted as soon as the ordinary procedure can operate. This authority ends upon Minimum Governance or the Founder’s departure from membership, whichever occurs first.

These engagements do not appoint Independent Review Panel members or alternates or confer Panel governance powers. They cannot be used to select or replace a reviewer to control a personal investigation, removal, compensation matter, or dispute.

Accountability and Continuity

Regular members and alternates may be removed during a term only for Just Cause. The following grounds constitute Just Cause.

  1. Loss of required attorney licensure, professional standing, or independence
  2. Fraud, dishonesty, corruption, or material misrepresentation relevant to service
  3. Serious or repeated breaches of confidentiality, conflict-disclosure, or recusal duties
  4. Substantial failure or refusal to perform assigned duties, including repeated unjustified missed deadlines
  5. Harassment, retaliation, or other serious misconduct connected to Panel service

Good-faith findings, dissent, and refusal to follow improper instructions are not cause. Allegations alone establish no finding. Correctable performance failures require notice and a reasonable opportunity to improve; serious misconduct does not require that opportunity.

Provide written allegations, proposed grounds, and supporting evidence at least fourteen calendar days before the removal hearing. The member may respond, present relevant evidence, be heard, and have an adviser or attorney. Removal requires three-fourths of all Disinterested Voting Directors and the required Board Quorum.

Interested Directors shall not deliberate or vote, and the affected member cannot decide their own case. Record specific findings, the vote, and effective date. Deliver the written decision to the member and preserve it in corporate records.

Temporary protective suspension requires documented grounds that continued participation threatens people, evidence, confidential information, or an active proceeding’s integrity. Approval requires three-fourths of all Disinterested Voting Directors and the required Board Quorum. Provide prompt written grounds and an opportunity to respond within seven calendar days.

Suspension expires after thirty calendar days unless lifted earlier or removal is completed. One extension of up to thirty days requires documented continuing necessity and the same vote. No further suspension on the same grounds is permitted.

Participation resumes automatically at expiration unless removal has been completed. Suspension establishes neither misconduct nor Just Cause.

An eligible alternate substitutes only when a regular member cannot participate. Substitution does not increase the Panel’s five voting seats. An alternate who begins substantive review shall complete that matter unless a conflict or inability to serve requires replacement.

Vacancies shall be filled through the ordinary appointment procedure for the remainder of the seat’s term. An eligible alternate may cover a vacant regular seat pending appointment, without automatically receiving permanent appointment.

An eligible regular member or alternate whose term expires may complete an already assigned review but accept no new matters without renewal. The outgoing member and successor cannot participate in the same voting seat on that matter. Removal, suspension, conflict, or inability to serve prevents continued participation.

Regular members and alternates follow the same Temporary and Permanent Incapacity standards and procedures applicable to Directors. Temporary Incapacity does not create a vacancy or establish Just Cause. Permanent Incapacity creates a vacancy under those shared procedures.

If no authorized, conflict-free body can act, required voting thresholds and prohibitions on self-review remain in effect. Available court relief may be sought as a last resort through a person legally entitled to apply.
