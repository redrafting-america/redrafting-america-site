Opening

PREAMBLE

In the summer of 1787, a small group of delegates in Philadelphia wrote a constitution they knew was imperfect. On the day it was signed, Benjamin Franklin agreed to it “with all its faults.” So they wrote Article V, a way to correct those faults, and left the correcting to the generations that followed. The Great Seal had already named what they hoped to build.

EPILOGUE

These Bylaws have spent their formidable length answering both questions in the language they require throughout. That means triggers and thresholds, funds and filings, offices and obstacles.
