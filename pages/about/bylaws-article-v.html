Article V

Leadership Structure

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article V introduces the Eleven Pillars of Leadership. Each Pillar Director holds a Board seat and leads a department, and the two cannot be separated. You do not get the vote without the work. Each Pillar protects something irreplaceable, and each Director is given one word to live up to: compass, bridge, historian, watchdog, guardian, ambassador, teacher, steward, conscience, architect, engine. Beside them sit three Independent Directors who lead nothing, so they can judge what others cannot. Some decisions cannot be judged by the people they concern. Delegation is allowed. Disappearing is not.

Director Positions and Corporate Officers

Before Mission Fulfillment Conversion, the Board of Directors has fourteen authorized positions: eleven Pillar Directors and three Independent Directors. Each open Pillar represents a distinct organizational function. Each Pillar appointment confers Board membership, the corresponding Corporate Officer office, and departmental responsibility. Every Corporate Officer must be a Pillar Director. After conversion, the Mission Fulfillment Board provisions establish the continuing positions and offices.

Independent Director appointment confers full voting Board membership and independent oversight responsibilities without a Corporate Officer office or departmental authority. Independent Directors share equal fiduciary responsibilities; their oversight includes audit, compensation, and related-party review. No Pillar Director may direct, supervise, evaluate, or coordinate their independent duties.

Every Director shall serve in an authorized position, perform its assigned duties, and remain accountable to the Board of Directors. Every Director owes fiduciary duties to the Nonprofit as a whole. Voting authority is equal except where these Bylaws expressly provide otherwise. Individual authority must arise from these Bylaws, Board delegation, or law. Pillar Directors shall implement lawful Board policies, resolutions, and directives.

Multiple Pillar positions may be held during formation or under Board-approved acting appointments following a resignation. Acting service shall not exceed one year. Each occupied Pillar carries one vote and counts once for Quorum and governance thresholds, even when one Director holds multiple Pillars. Simultaneous Pillar and Independent Director service remains prohibited. Temporary arrangements shall not merge or discontinue positions or diminish their distinct responsibilities, records, fiduciary duties, or legal obligations.

After formation, vacancies shall be filled as soon as reasonably practicable under the applicable appointment procedures and Governance Lifecycle deadlines. A vacancy does not change the Board of Directors’s authorized composition or another position’s existence, authority, or responsibilities. It does not excuse Quorum, voting, governance, or legal requirements.

Eleven Pillars of Leadership

The Eleven Pillars of Leadership are the permanent stewardship responsibilities of the Nonprofit. Every Pillar Director is entrusted with safeguarding one Pillar while sharing equal responsibility, together with the Independent Directors, for governing the organization as a whole. Figure 5.2 summarizes the Eleven Pillars, their Director-Officers, and their departments.

These are not merely eleven organizational positions. They are the Eleven Pillars of Leadership upon which the governance and mission of the Nonprofit rest.

Each position protects something irreplaceable.

The following job descriptions cover each of the Eleven Pillars. For every seat it gives a motto, a mission, a role, a line of accountability and the duties. Each ends with a single word for what that Director is: compass, bridge, historian, watchdog, guardian, ambassador, teacher, steward, conscience, architect, engine. A Director owns the outcome of each duty and answers to the Board of Directors for it. Delegation is allowed; disappearing is not. Four recurring reviews live here too: of the CEO, of legal compliance, of the Board of Directors itself, and of these Bylaws.

A Director’s responsibility for an assigned duty is a responsibility for its outcome and an accountability owed to the Board of Directors. It does not require the Director to personally perform every task. A Director may delegate execution to qualified staff, specialized outside counsel or contractors, or subordinate teams operating under the Director’s direction and supervision. The Director remains ultimately accountable to the Board of Directors for the result.

President (CEO)

Motto: Leadership Through Service

Mission and role: The President serves as Chief Executive Officer (CEO), translating Board direction into organizational action. The CEO advances the mission, founding principles, and Veritas Super Omnia — “The Truth Above All Else.” The President remains accountable to the Board of Directors while exercising independent judgment in daily administration.

  1. Board leadership. Preside over Board meetings, prepare agendas with the Secretary, and facilitate respectful, transparent, informed deliberations that protect every Director’s independent judgment.
  2. Executive management. Implement lawful Board policies, resolutions, and directives; supervise and coordinate executive work by Pillar Directors, committees, staff, volunteers, contractors, and other authorized personnel. This authority excludes directing, supervising, evaluating, or coordinating Independent Directors’ oversight duties, individually or through their committees.
  3. Public leadership. Represent the Nonprofit and serve as spokesperson subject to the Communications and Civic Engagement role once filled. Cultivate relationships with educational institutions, government, nonprofits, donors, media, and the public; advance the educational mission through communication and civic engagement.
  4. Instruments and compliance. Execute authorized contracts, grants, correspondence, and other legal instruments. Ensure compliance with governing documents, law, the Code of Ethics, Board policies, and founding principles.
  5. Continuity and reporting. Lead during emergencies, transitions, constitutional challenges, and other extraordinary circumstances (unusual, compelling conditions materially affecting the Nonprofit’s charitable mission or legal obligations); keep the Board of Directors informed of material developments. Deliver an annual State of the Mission Report covering strategy, education, finances, technology, ethics, public engagement, and coming priorities. Perform other lawful duties assigned by the Board of Directors consistent with these Bylaws.

Annual CEO evaluation. After Minimum Governance has continued for one year, the Board of Directors shall establish a schedule for CEO performance evaluations. It shall document a written evaluation at least annually. This requirement does not apply during Formation Governance. The President may provide information but shall not participate in evaluation deliberations or determinations. Evaluation does not alter appointment, compensation, removal, or Just Cause requirements.

Purpose: The President is the compass—grounding the mission, strategy, and public leadership of ReDrafting America in truth, constitutional principles, and the public trust.

Vice President (DEO)

Motto: Continuity Through Leadership

Mission and role: The Vice President serves as Deputy Executive Officer (DEO), supports the President, advances Board-approved priorities, and prepares to maintain executive leadership through absences and transitions. As the second-ranking executive, the Vice President remains independently accountable to the Board of Directors. Executive rank confers no additional Board authority or supervision over Independent Directors’ oversight duties.

Leadership expectations: Demonstrate judgment, integrity, intellectual humility, sound temperament, and commitment to the charitable mission, Foundational Principles, and constitutional vision. Place the Nonprofit’s long-term interests above personal loyalty, preference, or political influence; encourage evidence-based decisions, constructive disagreement, and institutional improvement.

  1. Continuity. Serve as Acting President when required by the governing succession procedures, until a successor is seated or the President lawfully resumes office. Regularly shadow the President’s duties to prepare for uninterrupted leadership.
  2. Institutional relationships. Cultivate relationships with key partners, strategic allies, and leadership contacts, distinct from routine public communications. Represent the Nonprofit when delegated by the President or authorized by the Board of Directors.
  3. Strategic initiatives. Lead occasional special initiatives assigned by the President or Board of Directors. Ongoing operational coordination remains with Operations and Efficiency.
  4. Leadership development. Assist with succession planning, leadership development, and Director onboarding. Help mediate Director disputes when requested by the President or Board of Directors, respecting independent oversight responsibilities. Perform other duties assigned by the Board of Directors consistent with these Bylaws.

Purpose: The Vice President is the bridge—ensuring continuity of leadership and strengthening collaboration across the organization. The Vice President prepares the next generation of leaders to carry the mission forward.

Secretary (CGO)

Motto: History Through Accuracy

Mission and role: The Secretary serves as Chief Governance Officer (CGO), preserving institutional memory, official records, and the integrity of governance processes. Accountable to the Board of Directors, the Secretary shall maintain complete, accurate, secure records in accordance with these Bylaws and law.

  1. Meetings. Record minutes documenting attendance, motions, votes, recusals, and significant deliberations. Prepare agendas with the President and distribute required notices and supporting materials, including those for Independent Director appointments, reappointments, and removal proceedings.
  2. Corporate records. Preserve governing documents, resolutions, committee reports, policies, and historical archives. Certify records and organizational documents for filings, grants, contracts, and other official purposes.
  3. Director records. Maintain appointment, term, onboarding, annual disclosure, conflict-of-interest, and ethics-acknowledgment records for both Pillar and Independent Directors. Preserve required independence reviews and track the Independent Directors’ staggered terms.
  4. Transparency and continuity. Ensure publication of non-confidential minutes, resolutions, annual reports, and other public governance records under transparency policies. Coordinate annual review and publication of governing documents; maintain amendment histories and preserve the reasons for decisions to inform future Boards.
  5. Governance support. Provide administrative and recordkeeping support without directing or supervising Independent Directors’ oversight duties.
  6. The Archive. Prepare The Archive for operation and lead it after Mission Fulfillment Conversion, preserving record integrity, custody, and permanent public access. Perform other duties assigned by the Board of Directors consistent with these Bylaws.

Purpose: The Secretary is the historian — preserving accountability and ensuring procedural justice.

Treasurer (CFO)

Mission and role: The Treasurer serves as Chief Financial Officer (CFO), safeguarding financial integrity, sustainability, and public trust. Accountable to the Board of Directors, the Treasurer shall maintain accurate financial records and sound fiscal management and provide information for Board decisions. The Treasurer shall ensure compliance with governing documents, law, and generally accepted accounting principles where applicable.

Publish an Annual Public Financial Report explaining financial position, major revenue sources, significant expenditures, and stewardship of donated resources.
  5. Risk and records. Identify financial risks; advise the Board of Directors on fiscal strategy, reserves, investments, and sustainability; recommend corrective action. Preserve complete, accurate records under law and the Technology and Records Standards. Perform other duties assigned by the Board of Directors consistent with these Bylaws.

Purpose: The Treasurer is the watchdog — stewarding our resources with integrity and public visibility.

Director of Legal Affairs and Compliance (CLO)

Motto: Justice Through Wisdom

Mission and role: The Director of Legal Affairs and Compliance serves as Chief Legal Officer (CLO). The CLO is accountable to the Board of Directors for legal and constitutional integrity across the assigned responsibilities. Provide or supervise objective legal advice, protect the Nonprofit’s interests, and engage specialized outside counsel as needed. This supervisory authority does not extend to the Independent Review Panel’s assigned responsibilities or Independent Directors’ oversight.

  1. Advice and instruments. Advise on nonprofit governance, corporate and constitutional law, tax-exempt compliance, intellectual property, contracts, employment, and data governance. Also advise on First Amendment protections, nonprofit advocacy, public education, and civic engagement. Review and draft governing documents, policies, agreements, and other legal instruments.
  2. Publications and protection. Support legally coherent, consistent, clearly written Constitution v2.0, Companion Guides, White Papers, and other official works. Protect intellectual property and other legal rights. Monitor legal changes, recommend policy adjustments, and develop strategies addressing litigation, regulatory, cybersecurity, and other legal risks.
  3. Investigations and proceedings. Provide legal support for ethics investigations, whistleblower protections, conflicts, and governance reviews while preserving independent oversight. Assist with disputes, government inquiries, audits, subpoenas, and official proceedings. Perform other duties assigned by the Board of Directors consistent with these Bylaws.
  4. Litigation authority. Commence, defend, prosecute, compromise, or settle proceedings with prior Board authorization. Prior authorization is unnecessary to defend proceedings against the Nonprofit or comply with legal process. It is also unnecessary for protective action permitted by the Legal and Regulatory Emergency procedures. These exceptions apply pending Board consideration at the earliest practicable meeting. Settlements admitting liability require approval by a majority of all eligible Disinterested Directors. The same requirement applies to settlements involving a Director, Corporate Officer, or Member as a party in that capacity.

Periodic legal-compliance review. After Minimum Governance has been maintained continuously for one year, the Board of Directors shall establish a recurring review schedule by resolution. The Chief Legal Officer shall coordinate reviews and report results with applicable privilege and confidentiality preserved. This additional review requirement does not apply during Formation Governance. It never postpones legal compliance, existing duties, filing deadlines, or correction of known violations.

Purpose: The Director of Legal Affairs and Compliance is the guardian—protecting the legal foundation, constitutional integrity, and institutional resilience of ReDrafting America. The mission may then endure regardless of changing political, legal, or social circumstances.

Independent Director Governance

Purpose. Certain decisions cannot be judged by the people they concern. Before Mission Fulfillment Conversion, the three Independent Directors hold no Corporate Officer office and lead no department. They exercise disinterested judgment where Pillar Directors, individually or collectively, cannot be presumed disinterested. Those matters include Pillar Director compensation, the financial statements, and audit findings. They also include related-party transactions, whistleblower reports concerning senior leadership, and Just Cause for removal of the Founder. The Nonprofit adopts this three-seat structure by choice as an enhanced safeguard. Applicable law mandates neither three Independent Directors nor this committee design. The structure is intended to support reliance on the rebuttable presumption of Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6. It is also intended to ensure that Directors who are in fact independent take committee action binding the Nonprofit under 15 Pa.C.S. § 5731.

Independent Directors share governing responsibilities regardless of their individual specialties. A specialty does not confer additional authority or excuse participation in other assigned oversight duties.

Seat 1, Seat 2, and Seat 3 identify initial scheduled terms of one, two, and three years, respectively. Subsequent terms last three years. Specialties are not assigned permanently to seat numbers. The Secretary shall record each appointee’s primary expertise separately from the seat designation.

Each Independent Director is a full voting member of the Board of Directors. Each holds all rights, duties, and fiduciary obligations of a Director under Pennsylvania law and these Bylaws. These are not generic Board seats. They are defined, function-bound seats governed by the Independent Director rules.

Independent Director Responsibilities

Independent Directors share responsibility for governing the Nonprofit and perform the following duties through the applicable Board of Directors, committee, and review procedures. Each shall act only when eligible and disinterested, disclose actual, potential, or reasonably apparent conflicts, and recuse when required.

  1. Compensation and related parties. Constitute the Compensation and Related-Party Committee, exercising its assigned final authority over Director and Officer compensation and related-party matters under the compensation rules.
  2. Financial oversight. Constitute the Audit Committee. Review financial statements, the independent examiner’s independence, examination findings, and internal controls. Track management’s corrective actions and report unresolved concerns and recommendations to the Board of Directors. The Treasurer manages finances, qualified external examiners conduct independent examinations, and the Board of Directors retains its assigned financial decision authority.
  3. Leadership complaints. Receive designated whistleblower and leadership complaints. Coordination of the required independent review follows the Chain of Authority. Notify the remaining Disinterested Directors when required. Preserve confidentiality, due process, and protection against retaliation.
  4. Leadership accountability. Participate when eligible in investigations, disciplinary proceedings, and removal decisions involving the Founder, Member, or other leadership under the applicable accountability procedures.
  5. Deadlock resolution. Provide recommendations on referred Required Matters and, when expressly authorized after a second tied vote, act as a special committee to resolve the matter. Final committee action requires at least two eligible Independent Directors and shall not decide matters reserved to the Board of Directors.
  6. Incapacity procedures. Receive qualifying referrals and participate in physician selection under the Chain of Authority and applicable incapacity procedures. Medical conclusions remain with the physicians.
  7. Emergency custody reports. Receive required reports of transfers of emergency access materials during investigations. This reporting role confers no custody or access authority.

A vacancy, recusal, or other inability to act does not waive applicable Quorum or approval requirements. Conflict-based reassignment follows the Chain of Authority and the authority expressly granted for the matter.

The Independent Review Panel cannot supply missing Board votes or Quorum. Its advisory assignments confer no final approval authority; binding decisions require express authorization under these Bylaws.

Independence Requirements

An Independent Director must remain independent throughout service. Eligibility excludes:

  1. Anyone who served as a Pillar Director, Corporate Officer, or compensated employee with the Nonprofit within the preceding five years.
  2. An Immediate Family Member of the Founder or a serving Pillar Director.
  3. Anyone with a financial interest in a Nonprofit transaction, contract, or compensation arrangement, except a permitted Independent Director stipend.
  4. A disqualified person under Internal Revenue Code § 4958 and its implementing rules, except solely by reason of Board service.

During service and for five years afterward, an Independent Director shall not accept a Pillar Director role with the Nonprofit. The same restriction applies to another compensated position or contract with the Nonprofit. No Pillar Director, including Ethics and Oversight, may direct, supervise, evaluate, or coordinate Independent Directors’ oversight duties. Independence does not excuse disclosure of actual, potential, or reasonably apparent conflicts or required recusal.

Terms and Staggering

Independent Director terms follow a fixed calendar beginning on the effective date of the first Independent Director appointment. Initial scheduled expirations are: (a) Seat 1, the first anniversary; (b) Seat 2, the second anniversary; and (c) Seat 3, the third anniversary. Each seat’s subsequent scheduled terms last three years, preserving one regular expiration each year.

The calendar continues while a seat is vacant. An initial appointee seated later, or a replacement appointed during a scheduled term, serves only its remaining portion. No appointment or vacancy restarts or extends the schedule. The Secretary shall record each seat’s scheduled expiration and include it in appointment and advance-resignation records.

Each initial or partial term counts as one term, permitting one subsequent three-year renewal. After two consecutive terms, an Independent Director must remain out of Independent Director service for at least one year before reappointment. No person may serve more than six consecutive years. This service break does not shorten the separate five-year restrictions on moving between Independent Director service and Pillar or compensated roles.

Reappointment is subject to the independent review and appointment procedures and is not automatic. Every appointment and reappointment requires the applicable advance term-expiration resignation. Appointment, onboarding, and the acceptance-and-resignation sequence remain prerequisites to exercising Director authority; the calendar alone confers no office or authority.

Service and Stipends

Independent Directors may receive a reasonable, fixed annual stipend for Board of Directors and committee service. The stipend shall be established before appointment and shall not depend on performance, revenue, organizational outcomes, or Pillar compensation.

The disinterested Independent Review Panel shall determine the stipend through its lawfully authorized compensation approval procedures. Approval requires at least four eligible participants and three affirmative votes. Independent Directors and anyone with a personal or financial conflict shall not participate in the decision.

Before approval, the Independent Review Panel shall obtain and rely upon appropriate compensation comparisons. It shall contemporaneously document the evidence, reasons, participation, recusals, and decision. The arrangement shall satisfy the applicable requirements for the federal rebuttable presumption of reasonable compensation before payment begins.

The Independent Review Panel’s stipend determination is final within its assigned authority. The Board of Directors or Independent Directors may request reconsideration but shall not revise the amount. Independent Directors retain authority over Pillar compensation under the compensation rules. Reasonable, documented business expenses remain reimbursable under the expense rules.

Removal Protections

An Independent Director may be removed only for cause through the Leadership Accountability procedures, including investigation and due process. The Member has no removal authority. Removal requires the unanimous affirmative vote of all serving Directors eligible to decide the matter. The Founder, Member, Acting Member, affected Independent Director, and anyone otherwise required to recuse shall not participate in the decision. They shall not count toward its voting threshold. Applicable Quorum requirements remain in effect.

The removal vote shall occur at a meeting for which notice was delivered by Confirmed Receipt at least fourteen days beforehand. A removal voted without that notice is void. The special protections governing Independent Directors during a Protected Founder Review remain in effect.

Founding Director Seat

While serving as a Director in Good Standing in any Pillar Director position, the Founder shall be recognized as the Founding Director. The Founder shall serve as the permanent steward of the organization’s founding mission. The Founding Director Seat is whichever Pillar Director position the Founder then occupies, as the Founder may relocate under the Founder Reassignments rules. It is included within the eleven Pillar Director positions and does not create an additional or twelfth position. Its permanence consists of the Founder’s protected tenure. The Founding Director may not be removed without Just Cause established through the Leadership Accountability procedures.

These protections exist solely to preserve continuity of mission, safeguard the organization’s founding principles, and prevent material deviation from its Charitable Purpose. They are intended to preserve the integrity of the organization rather than to confer personal privilege upon any individual.

Current service in the Founding Director Seat ends upon resignation, required retirement, death, Permanent Incapacity, or lawful removal for Just Cause. A living Founder who leaves service other than by removal for Just Cause holds Founder Emeritus status. Eligibility to return remains subject to the applicable maximum age and other eligibility requirements. Just Cause removal permanently extinguishes the Founding Director designation, protected tenure, and return rights. Death ends the ability to exercise personal rights.

A living Founder Emeritus never removed for Just Cause may return by written notice to a vacant, still-authorized Pillar position under the Founder-return rules. Discretionary Board approval is not required, but ordinary return cannot reopen a Pillar position permanently closed through Mission Fulfillment Conversion. Applicable age, qualification, medical-recovery, legal-record, disclosure, and onboarding requirements remain in effect. Prior Director service exempts only the Governance Examination. The Board may deny return for failure to meet those requirements or for Just Cause established through the applicable procedures. Member powers cannot override a valid denial. Recall following Catastrophic Board Loss remains a separate procedure.
