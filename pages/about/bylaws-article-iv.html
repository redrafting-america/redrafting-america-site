Article IV

Board Nomination and Election

Status: These Bylaws are pending.

Article IV is about how someone earns a place at this table. A name is put forward. The candidate discloses anything that could stand in the way of serving. Then comes a conversation with the sitting Board before anyone votes. Some things may never be the reason to choose a person: not a donation, not a famous name, not a political leaning. What counts is character, judgment, and the lived experience a person brings. Renewal is never automatic, and nobody sits in the room while their own future is being discussed. A seat here is a public trust. It is lent, never given.

Nomination Procedures

Candidates may be proposed for Pillar Director or Independent Director service. Each nomination shall identify the category and position sought and follow the applicable nomination and appointment procedures. During formation, the Founder may appoint qualified Pillar Directors but may not appoint Independent Directors.

Candidates shall provide the required qualifications, consent, disclosures, and background-screening information. Pillar nominations shall address departmental leadership; Independent Director nominations shall address independent oversight and the applicable independence requirements. No nomination guarantees appointment or may be exchanged for promises or benefits. The Secretary shall preserve nomination records.

Nomination Scope

These nomination procedures apply to both Pillar Directors and Independent Directors. Each nomination shall identify the category and position sought.

Shared requirements apply to all nominees. Nomination authority, qualifications review, and appointment shall follow the rules applicable to each Director category.

Nomination Authority

The Board of Directors may invite candidate recommendations from any source. A recommendation becomes a formal nomination only when submitted or endorsed by an eligible Director in Good Standing. Applicable formation or vacancy rules may provide exceptions.

During formation, the Founder may nominate and appoint qualified Pillar Directors by signed Board Resolution. Appointments shall be recorded and publicly disclosed under the Director transparency rules. After formation, Directors in Good Standing may nominate Pillar candidates, subject to the Member’s authority under the vacancy rules.

Eligible serving Directors in Good Standing may nominate Independent Director candidates in writing to the Secretary and the reviewers designated under the Chain of Authority. The Member and Acting Member may not nominate them in any capacity. The Board of Directors shall consider an Independent Director nomination only after receiving the required independent qualifications review.

Candidate Standards

The Board of Directors shall seek candidates with character, judgment, independence, relevant experience, and demonstrated commitment to public service and the charitable mission. It shall consider how each candidate strengthens the Board of Directors’s collective expertise, perspectives, backgrounds, and lived experience.

Pillar candidates should demonstrate qualifications relevant to the department they would lead. Independent Director candidates shall satisfy the independence requirements and demonstrate fitness for independent oversight.

Nomination Materials

Before formal review, each nominee shall consent in writing and submit:

  1. A biography or résumé identifying the Director category and position sought.
  2. A statement of interest, mission alignment, and relevant qualifications, including alignment with the Foundational Principles and Charitable Purposes.
  3. Contact information for at least two references able to address character, judgment, and qualifications.
  4. Disclosure of actual, potential, or reasonably perceived conflicts and outside roles, employment, financial relationships, political activity, affiliations, or commitments materially affecting independence, availability, eligibility, or fiduciary service.
  5. Confirmation that the nominee knows of no legal disqualification from nonprofit Director or Officer service.
  6. Willingness and ability to meet attendance, onboarding, disclosure, ethical, confidentiality, transparency, and fiduciary obligations.
  7. A current background check obtained and paid for by the nominee from a provider reasonably acceptable to the Board of Directors.
  8. Additional information reasonably required for a fair, informed evaluation.

Pillar nominees shall describe their qualifications to lead the department. Independent Director nominees shall provide disclosures and supporting information sufficient to verify the applicable independence requirements.

No nominee shall be required to disclose information irrelevant to Board service or prohibited from consideration by law. Candidate information shall be handled confidentially, except for required public disclosures.

Submission, Integrity and Records

Submit nominations early enough for meaningful review and distribute complete materials to every eligible participating Director before the selection meeting. Urgent vacancies may justify expedited processing but do not waive required screening, notice, Quorum, or appointment safeguards.

No nominee shall be promised a position, compensation, employment, preferential treatment, or other organizational benefit in exchange for accepting nomination, financial support, services, or relationships.

Directors shall disclose material conflicts concerning nominees and follow the conflict-of-interest and recusal rules. Friendship, professional acquaintance, or prior collaboration alone does not require recusal unless the relationship could impair independent judgment or create an appearance of undue influence.

The Secretary shall preserve nomination materials, disclosures, consents, and related records under the confidentiality, privacy, retention, and public-access rules. Category-specific appointment and vacancy procedures remain controlling.

Qualifications Review

Before election or appointment, each nominee shall undergo a fair, objective qualifications review using consistent standards and without unlawful discrimination. The review shall verify:

  1. Eligibility, required disclosures, and background screening.
  2. Relevant experience, judgment, integrity, and commitment to public service, the mission, and Foundational Principles.
  3. Ability to meet attendance, onboarding, confidentiality, fiduciary, and other service obligations.
  4. Actual, potential, or reasonably perceived conflicts affecting independence or effective service.

Candidates must satisfy the eligibility, qualification, disclosure, legal-record, background-screening, and other pre-service requirements applicable to their position. The Board of Directors or its authorized committee shall review Pillar nominees.

Before every Independent Director appointment or reappointment, a written independence and qualifications review shall be completed under the Chain of Authority. The review shall verify applicable independence and Director qualifications and identify actual or reasonably perceived conflicts. It is advisory and does not replace appointment authority.

Each Independent Director shall satisfy the general Director qualifications and applicable independence requirements. Each shall demonstrate relevant experience in at least one oversight area listed below.

Every Independent Director shall possess basic financial literacy and an understanding of fiduciary duties. Each shall demonstrate sound judgment, evidence-based reasoning, and the ability to challenge management respectfully. Each shall have sufficient availability for preparation, participation, and assigned oversight duties. Required onboarding and continuing conflict disclosures apply throughout service.

Collectively, the three Independent Directors shall provide expertise in:

  1. Financial oversight and audit, including financial statements, internal controls, audit findings, and financial condition
  2. Executive compensation and personnel oversight, including compensation comparisons, executive performance, and leadership complaints
  3. Nonprofit governance, ethics, and risk oversight, including conflicts, accountability, governance procedures, and identifying needs for specialized legal review

The qualifications review shall document each candidate’s experience and the expertise the appointment would contribute. Relevant professional credentials are preferred, but equivalent demonstrated experience may establish qualification. No particular professional license or governance credential is required solely for Independent Director service.

When all three positions are filled, their combined expertise shall cover all three oversight areas. A candidate may demonstrate expertise in more than one area. Recruitment and renewal reviews shall identify coverage gaps and seek to preserve complementary expertise. Expertise and independence shall be evaluated separately.

Political affiliation, religious belief, lawful personal viewpoints, protected characteristics, and other unrelated factors shall not be considered unless law expressly requires or permits consideration. When reasonably practicable, nominees shall have an opportunity to clarify discrepancies, address concerns, and provide additional information before a final recommendation.

The reviewer shall recommend in writing whether the nominee is qualified, qualified with noted considerations, or not qualified. The recommendation shall include reasons and only necessary personal information. The Board of Directors shall receive the required review before the selection vote. Qualification establishes eligibility for consideration, not entitlement to appointment.

The Secretary shall preserve review records under the confidentiality, privacy, retention, and public-disclosure rules.

Election and Appointment

After qualifications review, nominees shall be interviewed by the Board of Directors, subject to applicable participation exclusions and conflict safeguards. Eligible Directors shall receive complete materials sufficiently before the selection meeting and have a reasonable opportunity to ask questions and discuss qualifications. The applicable Quorum is required.

Directors shall exercise independent judgment in the Nonprofit’s best interests, considering integrity, public service, judgment, relevant experience, fiduciary fitness, mission alignment, and organizational needs. Pillar candidates shall demonstrate fitness to lead their department; Independent Director candidates shall demonstrate fitness for independent oversight. Selection shall not rest solely on donations, fundraising ability, influence, political affiliation, relationships, celebrity, employment, or other factors unrelated to fiduciary service.

Before Mission Fulfillment Conversion, all fourteen Director positions shall be filled individually through the applicable nomination, review, and appointment procedures. After conversion, each Mission Fulfillment Board position shall be filled individually through those procedures. Each appointment shall be separately considered and documented in the official minutes and a Board Resolution identifying the position and effective date.

Vacancies shall be filled through the procedures applicable to the position. Applicable Governance Lifecycle deadlines remain controlling.

  1. Pillar appointments. Except under applicable formation or vacancy procedures, a majority of the Eligible Seats nominates after the review and interview are completed and recorded. The Secretary records the vote and notifies the nominee. The Member may appoint by written instrument or decline, leaving the seat vacant for another nomination. Ordinary Pillar appointments are for one-year terms, subject to applicable vacancy and tenure rules.
  2. Independent Director appointments. Appointment or reappointment requires affirmative votes from at least three-fourths of eligible serving Directors. The Founder and any serving Member or Acting Member are excluded from participation, voting, and the number used to calculate the threshold. At least one eligible serving Independent Director must concur. No concurrence is required when no Independent Directors serve or none is eligible for the matter. Absence or refusal alone does not waive concurrence.
  3. Independent Director notice and review. Appointment and reappointment require a noticed meeting, with notice by Confirmed Receipt at least fourteen days before the vote. The required written independent review must be delivered to the Board of Directors and preserved before voting. Appointment or reappointment without the required review or notice is void.
  4. Prolonged Independent Director vacancies. Delay or Recorded Deadlock does not reduce the required vote or transfer appointment authority to remaining Independent Directors or the Constitutional Review Panel (CRP). Recruitment and seating deadlines follow the Governance Lifecycle rules.
  5. Taking office. The written selection action authorizes a conditional appointment. Where required, the Secretary shall receive the complete acceptance-and-resignation package and release acceptance first, establishing office and fiduciary duties. The advance resignation shall be released immediately afterward.

Voting, Quorum participation, and governing authority require confirmation of the completed sequence and certification of completed onboarding. Certification must cover all applicable requirements, including permitted emergency exceptions. Prior Director service exempts only the Governance Examination; all other applicable requirements remain in effect. Applicable term, renewal, and Acting Director procedures remain controlling.

Pillar Director Renewals

Pillar Director renewal requires a performance review, verification of continuing eligibility, updated disclosures, and a fresh background check. The Board of Directors shall consider whether another candidate would better serve the Nonprofit. The Director under review may provide information but shall not participate in deliberations or voting on their renewal.

Renewal requires final approval and receipt of the next term’s advance resignation before the current term expires. Otherwise, the existing resignation takes effect and the seat becomes vacant. Renewal is never automatic.

Scope and Principle

These renewal procedures apply only to fixed-term Pillar Directors. Independent Director reappointment follows the separate appointment and service rules, including independent screening, voting and concurrence, service limits, cooling-off periods, and advance resignation requirements.

Pillar Directors may serve successive terms without a numerical term limit while remaining in Good Standing and meeting all continuing qualifications. These include age, background-screening, and disclosure requirements. Each renewal must serve the Nonprofit’s best interests.

Renewal is not automatic. Before nomination for another term, the Board of Directors shall review attendance, preparation, participation, fiduciary performance, compliance, ethical conduct, and mission commitment. It shall also review continued ability to contribute to governance.

Annual Verification

For each annual Pillar Director renewal, the Board of Directors shall verify compliance with continuing governance requirements. The review should include, as applicable:

  1. Continuing eligibility for Director service.
  2. A current annual background check obtained and paid for by the Director from a provider reasonably acceptable to the Board of Directors.
  3. Updated disclosures of conflicts, outside affiliations, and other matters affecting service.
  4. The signed annual Conflict of Interest affirmation and other required annual acknowledgments or reaffirmations.
  5. Completion of required continuing education, governance training, and Board-required policy acknowledgments.
  6. Continued ability and willingness to fulfill the assigned Pillar’s responsibilities.

The Board of Directors may establish additional renewal requirements by resolution, consistent with these Bylaws and applied fairly and uniformly to similarly situated Directors.

Renewal Considerations

The Board of Directors may consider professional development, leadership, institutional knowledge, collaboration, committee service, ethical judgment, and responsiveness. It may also consider willingness to complete training and contributions to the Pillars of Leadership. It shall balance continuity with renewal and consider whether another qualified candidate would better serve organizational needs through new perspectives, expertise, or lived experience. Successful prior service creates no entitlement to renewal.

Before review, Directors shall disclose material changes since their last election affecting eligibility, qualifications, conflicts, professional obligations, availability, or ability to serve.

They may provide requested facts before withdrawing from deliberations, but shall neither influence nor vote on their own renewal. Each is excluded from the Eligible Seats and Quorum for that matter.

Record and Timeliness

The Secretary shall record the renewal decision in the minutes and preserve supporting documentation in corporate records. Renewal requires prospective Board approval and final documentation while the Director remains in office. Before the current term expires, the Secretary must also receive a personally signed advance resignation covering the succeeding term. Compliance with both requirements shall be recorded.

The succeeding term begins at the commencement stated in the renewal resolution. If either requirement is unmet at expiration, the existing advance resignation takes effect, service ends, and the seat becomes vacant without a removal vote. Later approval constitutes a new prospective appointment or election and cannot retroactively restore the prior term.

Public Accountability

ReDrafting America recognizes that public confidence in its governance depends upon openness, accountability, and the responsible stewardship of the public trust. Consistent with its charitable mission, the Nonprofit shall conduct Board nominations, elections, appointments, and renewals in a manner that promotes transparency. It shall respect legitimate requirements for confidentiality, privacy, and applicable law.

Following each election, appointment, or term renewal, the Nonprofit shall publicly identify every Director elected, appointed, or renewed. It shall publish the effective date of service and current Director position. It shall publish such biographical or professional information as the Board of Directors determines appropriate for public understanding of the Director’s qualifications and service.

The Secretary shall maintain accurate, current records of Directors’ appointments, resignations, removals, vacancies, terms, positions, responsibilities, and committee assignments. Records shall include other information required by these Bylaws or applicable law. The Board of Directors shall review all Directors’ names, positions, and responsibilities at least annually.

These records shall be published on the Official Website to the fullest extent consistent with these Bylaws, privacy obligations, and applicable law. Information lawfully withheld remains protected.

The Board of Directors may publish summaries of its nomination, qualifications review, election, appointment, and renewal procedures. Their purpose is to promote public confidence in the integrity, fairness, and independence of its governance practices. These public-accountability requirements do not require the disclosure of confidential background investigations, personal identifying information, protected personnel information, or privileged communications. Nor shall it require disclosure of other information lawfully withheld under these Bylaws or applicable law.

Directors remain accountable to the Board of Directors, the Nonprofit, and the public throughout their terms of service. A Director may no longer satisfy the qualifications established by these Bylaws, or may fail to fulfill the fiduciary obligations of office. A Director may materially violate the Code of Ethics or other governing policies, or otherwise become unable to serve effectively. Such a Director shall be subject to the applicable provisions governing resignation, removal, vacancies, discipline, or other corrective action.

The Board of Directors shall periodically review its nomination, election, appointment, renewal, and governance practices. The purpose is to ensure they continue to reflect the mission, Foundational Principles, and long-term interests of ReDrafting America. The Board of Directors shall adopt improvements whenever appropriate to strengthen transparency, accountability, fairness, and public confidence.
